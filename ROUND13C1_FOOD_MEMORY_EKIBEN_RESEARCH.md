# ROUND 13C.1 — FOOD MEMORY / EKIBEN RESEARCH

## Goal
Open the next high-visual Cultural Edition after Cultural Figure. The food category must use **real food photography and real archival/material references**. It must not become a monochrome geometry study with a food label placed on top.

## Candidate promoted for Pilot 01
**JAPAN / EKIBEN — STATION MEAL AS REGIONAL MEMORY OBJECT**

## Why this is a strong Food Memory pilot
Official JNTO material describes ekiben as boxed meals tied to rail travel and regional cuisine. The food is not detached from place: new regional railway connections historically helped produce regional ekiben traditions, and current ekiben still emphasize local specialties, seasonality and inventive packaging.

The National Diet Library has also documented bento as a long-running cultural form and explicitly identifies ekiben as regional railway-station meals. This gives us an institutional source for the broader bento/ekiben lineage, not only tourism imagery.

The Railway Museum in Saitama presents ekiben as a traditional food culture of Japanese railways, confirming that the meal belongs to a larger infrastructure/ritual context rather than functioning only as a dish.

## First promoted archive mechanisms
### 1. FOOD AS REGIONAL PORTRAIT
The visible meal becomes a compressed portrait of where the journey passes through or begins.

### 2. CONTAINER AS CULTURAL INTERFACE
Packaging is not neutral. Box layout, wrapping, labels, compartment logic and opening ritual are part of the food experience.

### 3. JOURNEY ACTIVATES THE MEAL
Ekiben is strongly associated with travel; the meal is consumed while the landscape moves past the window. The relation is therefore **food × place × passage**.

### 4. COMPARTMENTS AS MEMORY GRAMMAR
The physical organization of ingredients can create a native RELATIONAL KEY field: distinct components remain separate but form one meal only through the complete arrangement.

### 5. REGIONAL SERIES WITHOUT SKINNING
Different ekiben can be visually and structurally different because ingredients, packaging and local references change. This is useful for proving that Food Memory can generate a series without relying on one universal template.

## Proposed RELATIONAL KEY translation
### Working name
**EDIBLE PASSAGE**

### Product law
> **The journey does not decorate the meal. It completes its regional meaning.**

### Interaction candidate
**CLOSE → REGISTER → UNFOLD → REVEAL → TASTE / MEMORY**

Instead of a large permanent seam between two panels, the registration event could live in the **opening seam of the food container itself**.

Before registration:
- container mostly closed / partially concealed
- destination/region clue present but incomplete
- food image is not fully revealed

On valid relation:
- subtle registration mark resolves
- wrapping / lid / band opens
- full-color real food photography becomes visible
- regional trace/provenance is exposed only after the reveal

No-match:
- container remains a valid object
- opening ritual does not complete for that claimed region/context
- no red X, no fake food rejection

## Visual-source rule
**REAL FOOD FIRST**

Use:
- real photographs of ekiben
- real packaging where rights permit
- real station / journey / regional references
- public-domain, CC0, CC BY, or otherwise clearly usable sources for visible prototype material

Generated food is **not canonical final material** when a real usable source exists.

## Initial source/evidence set
- Japan National Tourism Organization — local ekiben culture: railway growth + regional cuisine + packaging + travel ritual.
- Japan National Tourism Organization — regional ekiben / travelling dishes.
- National Diet Library — 2019 exhibition material on bento, including ekiben as regional railway meals.
- Railway Museum — ekiben described as traditional Japanese railway food culture.
- Wikimedia Commons — multiple ekiben images, including recent CC0 examples suitable for prototype testing.

## Prototype visual target
This edition must be a strong contrast to Frida:
- high-saturation real food
- tactile rice / fish / pickles / vegetables / lacquer / wood / paper surfaces
- packaging and food compartments as native geometry
- minimal dark-site chrome around the object
- avoid ivory/black as the dominant food surface
- food should look edible before the user reads anything
