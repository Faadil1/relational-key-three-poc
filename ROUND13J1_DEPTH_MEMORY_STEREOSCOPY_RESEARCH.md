## Source-first basis

### Victoria and Albert Museum
The V&A describes stereographs as two photographs of the same subject taken from slightly different angles and mounted side by side. Viewed through a stereoscope, one image is presented to each eye and the pair merges into one three-dimensional scene.

Source: https://www.vam.ac.uk/info/collection-selection-boxes-photography-processes-and-techniques

### Science Museum Group
The Science Museum Group notes that Charles Wheatstone demonstrated the stereoscope to the Royal Society in 1838. The 3D effect is created by presenting two views of the same subject from different viewpoints so they merge perceptually.

Source: https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co152683/asher-law-stereoscope-london-england-1960-1968-stereoscope

### Real visual source
Public-domain stereocard of the 1871 International Exhibition in London, attributed to the London Stereoscopic Company. Copyright expired.

Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:InternationalExhibition1871Room6West.jpg

## Relational extraction

Historical mechanism:
**LEFT VIEW × RIGHT VIEW → BINOCULAR FUSION → DEPTH**

The two views are not supposed to become identical. Their controlled viewpoint difference is what makes stereoscopic depth possible.
