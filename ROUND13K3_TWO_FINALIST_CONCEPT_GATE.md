# FINALIST A — BOLIVIA / SIKU — IRA + ARKA

## Proposed edition

**MELODY MEMORY — BOLIVIA / SIKU — INTERLOCK REGISTER**

Core law:

# **COMPLEMENTARY ABSENCE PRODUCES CONTINUITY.**

Proof state:

`IRA ≠ ARKA → ALTERNATION → ONE SCALE / ONE MELODY`

## Card A

**IRA — COMPLEMENT A**

Role:
- carries one physically separate siku half;
- owns only its distributed subset of the demonstration note positions;
- remains visibly incomplete by design;
- never expands into the other card's notes.

The card should make absence legible without presenting absence as defect.

## Card B

**ARKA — COMPLEMENT B**

Role:
- carries the complementary physical siku half;
- owns the alternating note positions absent from Card A;
- remains a distinct instrument/object throughout the interaction.

Neither card is the melody by itself.

## Gap / relationship field

**MELODY INTERLOCK FIELD**

The center is not a waveform and not a resonance field.

It is a temporal/spatial score lane in which alternating note events from the two cards interlock. A continuous melodic route becomes visible only as Card A and Card B take turns occupying successive positions.

This avoids visual duplication of Ombak.

## Signature interaction

`NOTE → GAP → ANSWER → INTERLOCK → SCALE → MELODY → REGISTER → CONTINUE`

The sequence deliberately includes **GAP** because absence is part of the mechanism rather than an error state.

## Matching proof state

1. IRA produces the first illustrative note event from the left card.
2. The next note position remains visibly absent on IRA.
3. ARKA answers from the right card and occupies that missing temporal position.
4. Events alternate left/right without simultaneous near-unison beating.
5. The central score route fills progressively.
6. Only after both halves have contributed does one continuous melodic route become visible.
7. Registration preserves the separate authorship of every note event.

Proof:

# `A DOES NOT CONTAIN B → B DOES NOT CONTAIN A → A + B INTERLOCK → CONTINUITY`

The emergent information is the complete sequence.

## Other / mismatch semantics

Control label:
**OTHER INTERLOCK**

This is an editorial demonstration state, not a claim that a historical instrument is invalid.

Behavior:
- both cards still produce valid individual note events;
- timing / selected slots do not complete the target demonstration route;
- visible silent positions remain in the center;
- the system does not produce a red X and does not invalidate either instrument;
- result language: `TARGET PHRASE NOT COMPLETED · BOTH COMPLEMENTS REMAIN VALID`.

This preserves the collection-wide calm-resistance principle.

## Visual-source strategy

Primary anchor:
- public-domain image showing separated Ira and Arka halves (`Siku bipolar.jpg`).

Treatment:
- crop the real separated halves into Card A and Card B;
- preserve recognizable pipe lengths / material rather than redrawing generic panpipes;
- derive small note-position markers editorially but label them as an illustrative demonstration map;
- avoid costume, landscape or generic Andean decorative motifs unless independently sourced and necessary.

The cultural identity should come from the real instrument pair and its relational behavior, not ornamental styling.

## Sound strategy

Audio is required because the causal law concerns musical alternation.

Use restrained browser synthesis:
- one short tone at a time;
- left/right spatial placement corresponds to IRA / ARKA;
- no overlapping near-unison tones;
- no beat-frequency visualization;
- no claim of authentic siku timbre;
- no claim that the illustrative note sequence is a traditional melody.

The audio should prove alternation, not simulate ethnomusicological authenticity.

## Truthfulness boundary

Documented historical/cultural principle:
- separate complementary siku halves distribute the scale and are played in alternation / hocket to create one scale and melody.

Editorial translation:
- RELATIONAL KEY cards;
- chosen note-map positions;
- the exact demonstration phrase;
- central interlock graphics;
- synthesized tone timbre.

No claim is made that this UI, note map or phrase is historical.

## Static wireframe / interaction storyboard

### Frame 01 — DISTINCT
Two CR80 cards face each other across a dark narrow gap. Left contains the real IRA half; right contains the real ARKA half. Small inactive note sockets appear alongside the pipe rows. The gap contains an empty stepped timeline.

### Frame 02 — NOTE
One IRA pipe subtly wakes and emits one small note token into the first central slot. The next central slot stays empty.

### Frame 03 — ANSWER
An ARKA pipe activates from the opposite card. Its note token occupies the missing second slot. The eye immediately sees alternation rather than convergence.

### Frame 04 — INTERLOCK
Left/right events continue taking turns. A thin path joins completed adjacent slots, but each token keeps its source-side identity.

### Frame 05 — MELODY
The entire central route becomes continuous for the first time. No third card appears. The gap becomes meaningful only because both cards wrote into it.

### Frame 06 — REGISTER
The route settles. Small source markers remain alternating left/right to prove that continuity did not erase difference.

### Frame 07 — OTHER INTERLOCK
A different editorial sequence leaves two or more silent sockets unfilled. Both cards remain intact and active; the route simply never becomes continuous.

## Concept-gate verdict

**VERY STRONG.**

The two-object architecture is not invented by the interface. The cultural mechanism itself already distributes one musical system across two complementary physical instruments.

# FINALIST B — COLOMBIA / PASTO VARNISH MOPA-MOPA

## Proposed edition

**LAYER MEMORY — COLOMBIA / PASTO VARNISH — CUT-REVEAL REGISTER**

Core law:

# **A LAYER BECOMES INFORMATION THROUGH WHAT IT REVEALS BENEATH IT.**

## Card A

**UPPER MOPA-MOPA LAYER**

Role:
- carries a real-source varnish/color-layer material crop;
- behaves as the upper stretched sheet in the editorial translation;
- contains cuttable/reveal regions.

## Card B

**RECEIVING GROUND / LOWER COLOR LAYER**

Role:
- carries the lower receiving color/material field;
- supplies information visible only after overlay and cut/reveal.

## Gap / relationship field

**OVERLAP / CUT-REVEAL FIELD**

The two material fields approach and partially overlap in the center. A restrained cut path removes or opens selected upper regions so the lower layer becomes visible.

## Signature interaction

`HEAT → STRETCH → OVERLAY → CUT → REVEAL → REGISTER → CONTINUE`

## Matching proof state

- upper color sheet approaches;
- lower field remains stable;
- overlap forms only in the relationship field;
- cut windows open in the upper layer;
- lower color completes a selected composite pattern;
- the result is produced through layer relationship rather than color replacement.

Proof concept:

`UPPER LAYER + LOWER LAYER + SELECTIVE CUT → COMPOSITE REVEAL`

## Other / mismatch semantics

**OTHER LAYER**

- both materials remain valid;
- reveal windows expose a different lower field and do not complete the selected editorial composite;
- no red X;
- no claim that the craft object is wrong or invalid.

## Visual-source strategy

- use CC BY-SA / other reusable Pasto varnish imagery with required attribution;
- preserve real surface texture and cut/color behavior;
- avoid inventing a motif and presenting it as traditional;
- if an editorial pattern is needed, make its constructed status explicit.

## Truthfulness boundary

Documented process:
- heated / worked resin;
- pigmented thin sheets;
- application in layers;
- cutting / defining of material;
- multiple colors in the resulting decoration.

Editorial translation:
- two RELATIONAL KEY cards;
- the separated Card A/Card B roles;
- exact cut path and target reveal;
- interaction timing.

## Static wireframe / interaction storyboard

### Frame 01 — DISTINCT
Card A shows a glossy thin upper color layer. Card B shows a lower receiving color field. Gap is empty.

### Frame 02 — STRETCH
Card A material visually thins and extends toward the center without leaving the card entirely.

### Frame 03 — OVERLAY
A translucent overlap appears only in the relationship field.

### Frame 04 — CUT
A fine blade-like editorial path opens selected shapes in the upper layer.

### Frame 05 — REVEAL
Lower color becomes visible through the cuts and completes a composite image in the gap.

### Frame 06 — REGISTER
The overlap stabilizes while both source cards remain visible.

### Frame 07 — OTHER LAYER
The same cut windows reveal a different lower configuration; no selected composite resolves.

## Concept-gate verdict

**STRONG BUT SECOND.**

The material transformation is visually distinctive and would add a valuable non-sound family. However, the historical craft is fundamentally a layered process on a receiving object; splitting that process into two independent RELATIONAL KEY cards is an editorial construction.

The prototype can be truthful, but the pair is less native than Siku.

## Why Siku wins

1. The pair is culturally and physically native rather than reconstructed for RELATIONAL KEY.
2. The relationship cannot be reduced to one object without losing the documented mechanism.
3. Difference is not a residual to eliminate: each half intentionally lacks notes owned by the other.
4. The emergent outcome — one complete melodic continuity — exists only through alternation.
5. It extends the system beyond Ombak: **simultaneous controlled difference** becomes **distributed complementary absence**.
6. The mismatch state can remain calm and truthful: an editorial target phrase may remain incomplete without invalidating either instrument.
7. A public-domain image already shows the pair as two separated complementary objects.

## Pasto status

**PRESERVE AS HIGH-QUALITY RESERVE.**

Do not discard. It is an excellent candidate for a later South America balancing round or a future Layer Memory family, especially when the collection needs additional material/process diversity.
