## Authoritative source anchors

### British Museum — How to make a woodblock print like Hiroshige
https://www.britishmuseum.org/blog/how-make-woodblock-print-hiroshige

Documented process facts:
- kentō registration places printing paper exactly for each colour separation;
- two notches are used: corner `kagi` and long-edge `hikitsuki`;
- paper is aligned to these notches;
- the baren rubs the back of the paper so pigment transfers from the carved block.

### The Metropolitan Museum of Art — Woodblock Prints in the Ukiyo-e Style
https://www.metmuseum.org/ja/essays/woodblock-prints-in-the-ukiyo-e-style

Documented process facts:
- polychrome prints use a separate carved block for each colour, sometimes many blocks;
- two cuts on the edge of each block serve as alignment guides;
- the same paper must withstand repeated rubbing over successive blocks;
- mulberry-fibre paper was valued for this repeated-printing process.

### British Museum — baren OA+.7399
https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/A_OA-7399

Documented material fact:
- a baren applies pressure in a circular burnishing motion to transfer ink from woodblock to paper.
