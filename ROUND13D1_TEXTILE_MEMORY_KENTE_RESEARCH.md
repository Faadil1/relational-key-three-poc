## Research premise
The goal is not to apply a generic "African pattern" to a card. The visual and interaction grammar must be derived from documented textile construction.

### Source-grounded construction laws
1. Kente is woven as narrow strips on a loom rather than as one monolithic printed surface.
2. Completed cloths are assembled by sewing multiple independently woven strips edge-to-edge.
3. Warp-face and weft-face structures can coexist within a strip, creating directional contrast and registration.
4. Different Asante and Ewe traditions have specific histories and design systems; they must not be flattened into one decorative "African" aesthetic.
5. Some patterns/names can carry status, social or historical meaning. Do not invent meanings or reproduce restricted/reserved designs as generic decoration.

## Primary research sources
- The Metropolitan Museum of Art — Royal man's kente (prestige cloth), Asante-Akan, ca. 1910–1940. Documents narrow-band weaving and assembly of roughly twenty strips sewn edge-to-edge. The Met also exposes Open Access/public-domain material where applicable.
- The Metropolitan Museum of Art — Wrapper, Asante. Documents warp-face and weft-face patterning within strip-woven cloth.
- Smithsonian National Museum of African Art — Wrapper records. Documents named structural motifs, strip composition and provenance.
- British Museum — Ewe kente cloth records. Documents hand-woven strips sewn along selvedges and supplementary weft.

## Promoted interaction grammar
STRIP → ALIGN → INTERLACE → REGISTER → JOIN → CONTINUE

### Before
A CR80 card contains two or more textile strips that approach one another but do not yet form one continuous woven field.

### Match
The member claim and destination/context rule are represented by complementary strip structures. Registration occurs when the strip edges align and the directional warp/weft logic can continue without residual break.

### After
The seam becomes nearly invisible and the textile field continues across the card. The relationship is communicated by continuity of real woven material, not by an external icon or line.

### No-match
The strips remain slightly misregistered or offset. The textile itself is still valid; only this relational join does not resolve.

## Visual law
REAL TEXTILE FIRST → STRIP STRUCTURE → REGISTERED JOIN → CREDENTIAL CONTINUITY

## Anti-patterns
- no generic pan-African motifs
- no invented symbolic meanings
- no faux tribal pattern generation
- no blanket use of red/yellow/green as shorthand for Ghana
- no copying a named/reserved kente design and presenting it as RELATIONAL KEY's own pattern
- no replacing the card with a floating textile object
- no external oversized seam/junction

## Product opportunity
This is the first expansion where the core RELATIONAL KEY law can become nearly literal: a valid relation is a join between independently structured bands. The card remains identifiable as RELATIONAL KEY while the textile provides color, rhythm, tactility and cultural specificity.
