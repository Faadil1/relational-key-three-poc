# Finalist A — Hika Ahi

Pair:
- Card A — `HIKA / ACTIVE STICK`
- Card B — `KAUAHI / GROOVED BASE`
- Gap — `FRICTION / HEAT FIELD`

Canonical law selected for prototype:

# **SUSTAINED FRICTION PRODUCES EMBER.**

Canonical proof:

# `HIKA ≠ KAUAHI → FRICTION → HEAT → EMBER`

Why it satisfies the product invariant:
- the hika and kauahi remain visibly distinct objects;
- neither object contains an ember before relation;
- the emergent state appears at their interface;
- removing either object destroys the selected relation;
- registration does not merge the two objects into one.

Proposed signature:

`CONTACT → STROKE → TRACE → ACCUMULATE → HEAT → EMBER → REGISTER → CONTINUE`

### Matching state — TARGET GROOVE

Editorial interaction concept:
1. Hika and kauahi begin separated.
2. The active tip approaches a selected groove relation.
3. Repeated abstract contact passes leave a growing heat trace in the center field.
4. Wood-dust / heat indication accumulates editorially at the terminal relation point.
5. A small ember cue appears only after the complete target relation has been sustained.
6. `REGISTER` preserves both objects and the ember cue.

The number of UI strokes is purely editorial and must never be described as real-world ignition timing.

### Other state — OTHER CONTACT

- both tools remain valid;
- the selected contact path does not sustain the target groove relation;
- partial warmth / trace may appear editorially;
- no ember registers;
- no red X;
- the result is `RELATION NOT REGISTERED`, not `INVALID TOOL`.

### Safety / truthfulness boundary

This Cultural Edition is **not a fire-starting tutorial**.

Do not expose:
- exact stroke speed;
- exact duration;
- exact wood dimensions;
- moisture requirements;
- target temperature;
- tinder preparation instructions;
- operational survival guidance.

Historical/cultural fact is limited to the documented relational principle: hika ahi uses a fire-making stick rubbed in a groove of a base stick, generating heat through friction and allowing an ember / smouldering wood dust state to form.

All UI heat maps, stroke counts, glow intensities and timing are editorial translations.

### Visual strategy

Do **not** copy restricted Māori historical photographs.

Use an original source-grounded material rendering based on documented tool roles:
- slender active stick / hika;
- longer grooved base / kauahi;
- restrained wood material language;
- no generic Māori ornament or pan-Indigenous motifs;
- culture is carried by the named mechanism and relation, not surface decoration.

Authoritative references remain Te Papa / Te Ara / Science Museum Group.
