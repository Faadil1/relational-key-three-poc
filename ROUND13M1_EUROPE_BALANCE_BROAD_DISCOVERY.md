## 1. FRANCE — BOULLE MARQUETRY / PREMIÈRE PARTIE + CONTRE PARTIE

### Documented mechanism

In Boulle work, a thin sheet of tortoiseshell and a thin sheet of metal such as brass are placed together and cut through simultaneously according to one design. The layers are then separated. The cut pieces can be recombined as inverse versions: brass in tortoiseshell and tortoiseshell in brass. Historical furniture was often made in complementary pairs because the same shared cut yielded both versions.

### Candidate pair

- Card A — `PARTIE / GROUND A + INLAY B`
- Card B — `CONTREPARTIE / GROUND B + INLAY A`
- Gap — `SHARED CUT / COMPLEMENTARY EXCHANGE FIELD`

### Candidate law

**A SHARED CUT PRODUCES COMPLEMENTARY INVERSION.**

Proof candidate:
`A + B → STACK → CUT ONCE → SEPARATE → EXCHANGE → PARTIE + CONTREPARTIE`

### Why strong

- native two-material relation: VERY HIGH;
- one operation creates two reciprocal outcomes;
- the second object is not a copy but the material inverse of the first;
- visually pair-native and highly legible;
- strong public-domain visual path at The Met;
- materially distinct from Stereoscopy, Fès, Kento and Kente.

### Risks / boundaries

- historical Boulle work often uses tortoiseshell; implementation must treat that as historical evidence, not encourage present-day use of protected wildlife material;
- do not reduce the family to Baroque ornament / luxury styling;
- identity must come from `shared cut → inverse exchange`, not decorative scrollwork.

### Authoritative sources

- The Metropolitan Museum of Art — attributed to André-Charles Boulle, Cabinet: https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/202281
- The Metropolitan Museum of Art — Alexandre-Jean Oppenordt, Small desk with folding top: https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/207667
- Met public-domain pair / Boulle-work examples: https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/209080

## 2. ITALY / VENICE — RETICELLO GLASS / OPPOSING SPIRAL LAYERS

### Documented mechanism

Venetian reticello uses two superimposed layers of glass canes. The layers spiral in different directions; their overlap creates a network of crossing white threads. Characteristic trapped bubbles can appear at the intersections.

### Candidate pair

- Card A — `CLOCKWISE CANE LAYER`
- Card B — `COUNTERCLOCKWISE CANE LAYER`
- Gap — `CROSSING / NETWORK FIELD`

### Candidate law

**OPPOSING SPIRALS PRODUCE A NETWORK.**

Proof candidate:
`LAYER A ≠ LAYER B → SUPERIMPOSE → CROSS → NETWORK`

### Why strong

- emergent third pattern is immediately visual;
- both layers remain structurally meaningful;
- public-domain visual anchor exists at The Met;
- strong material novelty: glass / transparency / crossing layers;
- no dependence on optical depth or exact-match registration.

### Risks / boundaries

- the historical technique is multilayered glassworking, not literally two freestanding finished objects;
- must prove the two-card translation is not artificially splitting one object;
- avoid turning the center into a generic moiré effect.

### Authoritative sources

- The Metropolitan Museum of Art — Venetian reticello plate, public domain: https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/187390
- Corning Museum of Glass — Renaissance Venetian glassworking / reticello: https://renvenetian.cmog.org/chapter/18th-century-decline

## 3. FINLAND — SAUNA / LÖYLY

### Documented mechanism

UNESCO describes löyly as the spirit or steam released by casting water onto a stack of heated sauna stones. It is identified as being at the heart of Finnish sauna culture, which was inscribed on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2020.

### Candidate pair

- Card A — `WATER`
- Card B — `HEATED STONES`
- Gap — `LÖYLY / STEAM FIELD`

### Candidate law

**CONTACT BETWEEN WATER AND HEATED STONE RELEASES LÖYLY.**

Proof candidate:
`WATER ≠ HOT STONES → CONTACT → STEAM → LÖYLY`

### Why strong

- culturally exceptionally well grounded;
- third state is immediate and unmistakable;
- relation is environmental / atmospheric rather than optical or structural;
- highly distinct from Stereoscopy and from Hika Ahi despite both involving energy transformation.

### Risks / boundaries

- pair is asymmetrical: water is a material input, stones are a heated object/system;
- could become a simple cause-effect demo rather than two co-equal relational objects;
- implementation must avoid health claims and avoid turning into sauna operating instructions;
- visual rights for UNESCO imagery are not automatically reusable, so original rendering may be required.

### Authoritative sources

- UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage — Sauna culture in Finland: https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/sauna-culture-in-finland-01596
- UNESCO decision 15.COM 8.B.27: https://ich.unesco.org/en/decisions/15.COM/8.B.27

## 6. ITALY / FLANDERS — BOBBIN LACE / CROSS + TWIST

Documented mechanism:
Bobbin lace evolved from braiding. Thread is wound on bobbins; pairs of bobbins are crossed and twisted according to a pattern and secured with pins.

Candidate law:
**REPEATED CROSS + TWIST BUILDS A NETWORK.**

Strength:
- relational crossing is culturally grounded and tactile.

Downgrade reason:
- the actual system uses many bobbins/pairs rather than one canonical pair;
- textile territory is already occupied by Kente;
- Reticello offers a cleaner two-layer network law with less mechanism overlap.

Source:
- The Met — Gertrude Whiting’s Bobbin-Lace Sampler: https://www.metmuseum.org/fr/perspectives/gertrude-whiting-bobbin-lace-sampler
