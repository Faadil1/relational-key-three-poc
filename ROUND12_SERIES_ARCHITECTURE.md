# ROUND 12 — SERIES EXPANSION SYSTEM

## Status
ROUND 12A — SERIES THESIS + RESEARCH ARCHITECTURE

## Working series name
RELATIONAL KEY — CULTURAL EDITIONS

Working name only. Do not lock naming before the pilot set proves the series.

---

## 1. Series thesis

RELATIONAL KEY is no longer only one reciprocal-access credential. It is a collectible credential system in which a stable relational geometry can host different territorial memories, archives, everyday objects, foods, infrastructures, and cultural traces without reducing culture to decoration.

The system must always preserve the product law:

> The credential is half of the interface. Access is created by the relationship between member claim and destination rule.

And the visual law:

> A valid relationship completes the interrupted path and allows it to continue.

The Cultural Editions expansion adds a second principle:

> Culture is not a skin. Archival evidence must change how the credential is structured, encoded, sequenced, marked, or used.

---

## 2. Immutable core vs variable edition layer

### IMMUTABLE — every RELATIONAL KEY edition must retain

1. CR80-compatible physical credential logic.
2. Credential = one side of a relationship, never a self-sufficient key.
3. Destination = the other side of the interface.
4. Registered Continuation path.
5. Junction / registration event.
6. Positive and no-match states that preserve membership validity.
7. Explainability on demand.
8. Functional information hierarchy: issuer, member/tier, validity, claim/network, fallback verification.
9. Truthfulness boundary between real rules, simulated member state, prototype resolver, and conceptual integration.
10. Geometry must still identify the project when logos, names, and colors are removed.

### VARIABLE — an edition may transform

1. Path rhythm and segmentation.
2. Junction profile / registration marks.
3. Information bands and denomination logic.
4. Front/back hierarchy within functional constraints.
5. Color system.
6. Typography category and spacing logic.
7. Material / print treatment if manufacturable.
8. Edition numbering and collectible logic.
9. Metadata structure.
10. Motion response, only when derived from the edition mechanism.

### NEVER VARIABLE

The project cannot become a set of unrelated illustrated cards.

If an edition still works after removing the archive rationale and replacing it with any random graphic, reject it.

## 3. Archive → mechanism → translation protocol

Every edition must pass through five stages.

### A. ARCHIVE CORPUS
Collect a minimum viable corpus before visual design.

Preferred minimum for promotion:
- 12+ distinct objects or records,
- 2+ source families,
- at least 1 institutional / first-party source when reasonably available,
- enough temporal or typological variation to distinguish a system from a one-off style.

### B. MECHANISM EXTRACTION
Do not extract “looks.” Extract behaviors and systems.

Examples:
- repeated denominations,
- sequential stamps,
- route encoding,
- wear/use marks,
- serial correspondence,
- modular bands,
- service explanations,
- infrastructure as emblem,
- packaging hierarchy,
- ingredient/provenance ordering,
- fold/cut/perforation logic,
- collectible series behavior,
- identity through utility.

### C. CULTURAL CLAIM
State exactly what the corpus supports.

Good:
> A recurring packaging system uses denomination and category bands to distinguish product families.

Bad:
> This is what Mexican design looks like.

No edition may claim national or continental identity from a small commercial sample.

### D. RELATIONAL TRANSLATION
Convert the extracted mechanism into a RELATIONAL KEY behavior.

Examples:
- denomination family → membership tier bands,
- route stages → sequential relational path,
- stamp accumulation → participation evidence,
- paired print registration → claim/rule junction,
- ingredient hierarchy → provenance/explanation stack,
- package opening sequence → progressive disclosure,
- service explainer → front/back causal information.

### E. DISTANCE TEST
The final credential must be visibly transformed from the source material.

We borrow mechanisms, not layouts, illustrations, logos, copyrighted artwork, or cultural symbols as decoration.

## 4. Four research lanes per edition

Each territory / edition should explore all four lanes before choosing one or combining two.

### LANE 1 — CIVIC / UTILITY OBJECTS
Examples:
- transit passes,
- phonecards,
- library cards,
- ration cards,
- membership cards,
- postal objects,
- school credentials,
- public-service documents.

Question:
> How did a small object organize access, entitlement, identity, or public service?

### LANE 2 — EVERYDAY CULTURE / FOOD
Examples:
- packaging,
- labels,
- market receipts,
- menus,
- food tins,
- ingredient systems,
- grocery ephemera,
- recipe cards,
- trade labels.

Question:
> How did everyday objects encode category, provenance, trust, repetition, or desire?

### LANE 3 — PEOPLE / CULTURAL FIGURES
Do not default to portraits.

Prioritize traces:
- manuscripts,
- notebooks,
- scores,
- diagrams,
- signatures,
- tools,
- routes,
- objects,
- publications,
- stage/program ephemera,
- archival systems connected to the person.

Question:
> What repeatable system or trace from this person’s work can become a credential mechanism?

A portrait is allowed only when the likeness is genuinely necessary and rights/provenance are clear.

### LANE 4 — PLACE / INFRASTRUCTURE / PARTICIPATION
Examples:
- stations,
- markets,
- ports,
- public signage,
- pavilions,
- fairs,
- civic events,
- public networks,
- architecture used as system rather than illustration.

Question:
> How did people move, participate, connect, or prove presence within this place/system?

## 5. Source ladder

Rank sources by evidentiary strength.

### TIER A — FIRST PARTY / INSTITUTIONAL
Highest priority:
- national archives,
- official museums,
- transit agencies,
- telecom operators,
- libraries,
- cultural ministries,
- artist/person estates or foundations,
- institutional digitized collections.

### TIER B — SPECIALIST / CURATED
Useful for expanding a corpus:
- specialist archives,
- university collections,
- design museums,
- scholarly catalogues,
- documented private collections.

### TIER C — MARKETPLACE / COLLECTOR
Discovery only unless corroborated:
- auction listings,
- collector sites,
- eBay / Delcampe-like marketplaces,
- unsourced Pinterest / social posts.

Tier C may reveal objects or series, but cannot independently support a national/cultural claim.

## 6. Provenance labels

Each archive item stored in the research register must include:

- object title / description,
- territory,
- approximate date,
- source institution,
- source URL,
- source tier,
- rights / reproduction note if known,
- mechanism observed,
- confidence,
- whether the object is safe as visual reference, mechanism-only reference, or requires further verification.

Confidence labels:
- VERIFIED — institutional or strong primary evidence.
- CORROBORATED — multiple credible sources.
- DISCOVERY ONLY — marketplace / weak provenance.
- REJECTED — misleading, stereotypical, rights problem, or no usable mechanism.

---

## 7. Anti-cliché / cultural integrity gate

Reject an edition if any of these are true:

1. Territory is communicated mainly through a flag palette.
2. “Traditional pattern” is used without named provenance and context.
3. A whole continent is presented as one visual culture.
4. Sacred/religious material is turned into decoration without necessity/context.
5. A famous person is reduced to a portrait skin.
6. Food is used as a mascot rather than a system/provenance/ritual/market mechanism.
7. Archive objects are copied rather than transformed.
8. A colonial archive is treated as neutral evidence of local self-representation.
9. Marketplace objects are presented as authoritative national history.
10. The cultural layer compromises real credential usability.

Required counter-question:
> Could a person from the represented culture explain why this mechanism belongs here without relying on visual stereotype?

---

## 8. Rights / ethics gate

For each promoted edition:

- Prefer public-domain, open-access, or clearly licensed archive images for direct visual reproduction.
- If rights are unclear, use the source for mechanism study only.
- Never reproduce a copyrighted archive artwork as the credential face without permission.
- For living or recently deceased figures, separate factual/archival research from likeness usage and verify rights before publication.
- For sacred, ceremonial, funerary, Indigenous, or community-restricted material, require contextual review before use.
- Keep research credits in the case study even when final design is highly transformed.

---

## 9. Edition scoring rubric — 60 points

Each candidate edition is scored before design promotion.

### Evidence depth — /10
0–3 weak / marketplace-led
4–6 mixed evidence
7–8 strong curated corpus
9–10 strong institutional / multi-source corpus

### Mechanism distinctiveness — /10
Does the archive produce a mechanism we do not already have?

### Relational fit — /10
Can the mechanism naturally transform claim × rule → registration → continuation?

### Cultural specificity — /10
Is it specific enough to avoid continent-level cliché?

### Manufacturability / utility — /10
Can it remain a believable credential?

### Series contribution — /10
Does it expand the family rather than duplicate another edition?

Promotion threshold:
- 48+ = PROMOTE
- 42–47 = HOLD / deepen research
- under 42 = REJECT or archive as reference

No candidate can PROMOTE with Evidence depth < 7 or Cultural specificity < 7.

## 10. Initial verified evidence anchors

These are research anchors, not visual templates.

### STO Multi Jeunesse — Gatineau / Canada
Official STO documentation confirms:
- one shared Multi Jeunesse card is distributed to eligible secondary students,
- students are asked to write their name on it,
- the same card is kept through secondary school,
- it must still be tapped even when travel is free,
- it can carry paid fare products outside free-access periods.

Mechanisms worth studying:
- one common civic object + personal ownership,
- longitudinal belonging,
- free entitlement still requires a validation event,
- youth identity riding on a real utility core.

Status: VERIFIED / Tier A.

### London Transport Museum — Reinohl Collection
The museum describes 180 albums containing tickets, route notes, drawings, clippings, and transport ephemera, with tickets as the core and London Transport historically supplying samples of new tickets.

Mechanisms worth studying:
- long-running fare series,
- denomination and route families,
- operator/system continuity,
- tiny paper objects as dense service information.

Status: VERIFIED / Tier A.

### Expo 67 passport — Canada
Canadian Museum of History and McCord records confirm passport/pass objects from Expo 67. The Canadian Museum of History also documents the Expo identity system and promotional passport material.

Mechanisms worth studying:
- participation object,
- passport/entry logic,
- event identity distributed into a pocket object,
- participation accumulating through use.

Status: VERIFIED / Tier A.

### NTT magnetic telephone cards — Japan
NTT East distinguishes general telephone cards from designed/custom-made telephone cards and explicitly describes four-color custom cards used for advertising, commemorative gifts, anniversaries, openings, and announcements.

Mechanisms worth studying:
- stable utility core + flexible cultural/editorial skin,
- denomination/service type,
- commemorative seriality,
- mass infrastructure becoming collectible media.

Status: VERIFIED / Tier A.

### MODO — Museo del Objeto del Objeto, Mexico
MODO states its online database contains more than 10,000 records/images. Its Comestibles category currently exposes hundreds of food-related objects including packages, bags, tins, condiments and commercial ephemera.

Mechanisms worth studying:
- food category families,
- package hierarchy,
- retail identity,
- everyday objects as cultural memory,
- product provenance and denomination.

Status: VERIFIED / Tier A.

### Powerhouse Collection — Australia
Powerhouse documents historical food packaging and collections spanning multiple decades, including early twentieth-century packaging collections and later commercial packaging.

Mechanisms worth studying:
- temporal evolution within one object class,
- package information hierarchy,
- food/product family systems,
- collectible inserts and utility + media combinations.

Status: VERIFIED / Tier A.

### Nigeria — National Archives + NCMM
Nigeria's National Archives describes holdings spanning documentary records, indigenous governance, photographs, maps and personal papers. NCMM provides an official digital museum/collection searchable by culture, region, material and period.

Mechanisms worth studying:
- local-first documentary provenance,
- material classification,
- region/culture/period metadata as an alternative to generic national styling,
- infrastructure/history research that can corroborate weaker phonecard material.

Status: VERIFIED / Tier A.

### Benin — official cultural / archival infrastructure
The Government of Benin and Ministry of Culture document active national museum development and cultural heritage programs; the National Archives of Benin provides official archival infrastructure and digitized documents.

Current limitation:
The previously collected Benin phonecard corpus remains predominantly marketplace-led. It is valid for mechanism discovery but NOT yet authoritative national design history.

Requirement before a Benin Cultural Edition promotes:
- corroborate at least one mechanism through local institutional, telecom, archival, museum, or scholarly sources,
- distinguish personal memory / collector evidence from historical claims.

Status: INSTITUTIONAL CONTEXT VERIFIED; PHONECARD DESIGN CORPUS STILL DISCOVERY-LED.

## 11. Pilot-set strategy

Do NOT start with one generic card per continent.

The first pilot set should test six DIFFERENT mechanism families. Geography is secondary to mechanism diversity.

Target slots:

1. YOUTH / CIVIC MOBILITY
   - starting anchor: STO Multi Jeunesse

2. COMMUNICATION / INFRASTRUCTURE
   - starting anchors: Benin + Nigeria phonecard / telecom research, with stronger provenance work required

3. PARTICIPATION / PASSPORT
   - starting anchor: Expo 67 / fair-participation objects

4. FOOD / EVERYDAY MEMORY
   - starting anchors: MODO Mexico + additional local food/market archive research

5. LONGITUDINAL TRANSIT / DENOMINATION SYSTEM
   - starting anchor: London Transport Museum Reinohl Collection

6. UTILITY CORE + CULTURAL SKIN / COMMEMORATIVE SERIES
   - starting anchor: NTT Japanese telephone cards

A seventh Oceania candidate may later use Powerhouse food/packaging archives if it produces a mechanism distinct from the Food edition.

These are research slots, NOT promoted final cards.

---

## 12. Geographic expansion rule

After the first six mechanism-diverse pilots, expand deliberately across:
- Africa,
- Asia,
- Europe,
- North America,
- South America,
- Oceania.

But the unit of research should usually be:
- city,
- country,
- community,
- institution,
- route/network,
- object family,
not “continent style.”

Continent is a collection-navigation layer, not a visual identity claim.
