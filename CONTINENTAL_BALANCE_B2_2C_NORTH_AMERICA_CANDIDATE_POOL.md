# N1 — JANNEY AUTOMATIC RAIL COUPLER / VIRGINIA, UNITED STATES

## Source grounding

Institutional/object anchor:
- Smithsonian National Museum of American History, Janney Coupler model / coupler collection.

Primary mechanical record:
- Eli H. Janney, U.S. Patent 138,405, `Improvement in car-couplings`, patented April 29, 1873.

Sources:
- https://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object/nmah_881750
- https://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object/nmah_841985
- https://patents.google.com/patent/US138405A/en
- USPTO Janney-type classification description: https://www.uspto.gov/web/patents/classification/uspc213/defs213.pdf

## Documented relationship

The Janney-type coupler uses mating coupler heads with pivoting knuckles. Contact between compatible coupler heads swings the knuckle toward closed position; a locking mechanism retains the coupled state. Janney's patent emphasizes close interlocking, automatic coupling behavior and a release lever for uncoupling.

## Gap-map territory

Primary: **R5 — Latch / Clasp / Discrete Engagement**
Secondary: **R4 — Load / Structural Transfer**

## Candidate law

# `MATING KNUCKLES TURN CONTACT INTO A LOCKED LOAD PATH.`

Signature:
`HEAD A ≠ HEAD B → APPROACH → KNUCKLES PIVOT → LATCH → LOAD TRANSFERS → RELEASE`

## Pair translation

- Card A = rail coupler head A;
- Card B = rail coupler head B;
- both carry their own knuckle geometry;
- approach causes actual mirrored engagement;
- latch creates a new structural state;
- release returns both valid couplers to separable states;
- `OTHER` can show both knuckles closed / incompatible approach geometry without declaring either coupler invalid.

## Strengths

- near-perfect two-member symmetry;
- engagement visibly changes system capability;
- source includes object evidence + original patent mechanics;
- entirely different from Gatineau and Coyoacán;
- center need only contain contact line, never a third object;
- visually strong without cultural ornament.

## Risks

- industrial rather than community/craft memory, so collection framing must explain why this is a North American relationship technology rather than merely generic machinery;
- avoid heroizing inventor biography; center the documented coupling law and rail-safety/transport system context;
- build must show structural engagement, not simply two icons touching.

# N2 — YALE PIN-TUMBLER LOCK / UNITED STATES

## Source grounding

Smithsonian National Museum of American History:
- 1861 lock patent model associated with Linus Yale Jr., Philadelphia, patent date May 14, 1861.

Mechanism corroboration:
- Yale historical documentation explains that pins of varying lengths prevent plug rotation until the correct serrated key lifts pin stacks to the required positions.
- Lock Museum of America preserves an original Yale pin-tumbler patent model and a Yale Lock Room.

Sources:
- https://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object/nmah_850663
- https://www.yaledws.co.uk/uk/en/about-us/history-of-yale/the-yale-pin-tumbler-invention
- https://www.lockmuseumofamerica.org/museum-rooms
- Smithsonian Yale trade literature: https://www.si.edu/object/trade-catalogs-yale-towne-mfg-co%3ASILNMAHTL_34459

## Documented relationship

The flat key's profile raises lower pins to positions that allow the cylinder plug to rotate relative to its outer housing. A non-corresponding profile leaves one or more pin stacks crossing the shear boundary.

## Gap-map territory

Primary: **R11 — Encode / Decode / Completion**
Secondary: **R5 — Discrete Engagement**

## Candidate law

# `KEY PROFILE POSITIONS PIN STACKS SO A CYLINDER CAN ROTATE.`

Signature:
`KEY ≠ LOCK → INSERT → LIFT PINS → SHEAR LINE → ROTATE → OPEN / WITHDRAW`

## Pair translation

- Card A = key / encoded edge profile;
- Card B = pin-tumbler cylinder / pin stacks;
- approach/insertion maps profile heights to pins;
- correct relation clears the shear boundary and permits rotation;
- OTHER key leaves visible residual crossings while key and lock remain valid objects for different counterparts.

## Strengths

- exceptionally clear encode/decode relation;
- highly legible alternate/mismatch semantics;
- strong source trail from patent model to mechanism documentation;
- distinct from every current collection family;
- interaction can be understood without cultural skinning.

## Risks

- lock/key is a familiar generic archetype, so the edition must be anchored to the documented Yale pin-tumbler innovation rather than presenting a timeless universal key metaphor;
- avoid cybersecurity/password metaphor creep; keep the physical mechanism primary;
- do not let the shear-line visualization become a third product/dashboard.

# N3 — MAYA CALENDAR ROUND / YUCATÁN + MAYA REGION

## Source grounding

Smithsonian National Museum of the American Indian `Living Maya Time` educational project documents the relation between:
- Tzolk'in / Chol Q'ij: 260-day cycle;
- Haab: 365-day cycle;
- Calendar Round: interweaving of the two cycles, with the same combination repeating after 18,980 days / 52 Haab years.

Sources:
- https://maya.nmai.si.edu/calendar/calendar-system
- https://maya.nmai.si.edu/sites/default/files/resources/The%20Maya%20Calendar%20System.pdf

## Documented relationship

A full Calendar Round date combines one state from each independent cycle. Their combined configuration repeats only after the least-common-multiple relationship of the 260- and 365-day cycles.

## Gap-map territory

Primary: **R9 — Cycle / Synchronization**
Secondary: **R11 — Encode / Decode**

## Candidate law

# `TWO INDEPENDENT CALENDAR CYCLES PRODUCE A DATE THROUGH SYNCHRONIZATION.`

Signature:
`260-CYCLE ≠ 365-CYCLE → ADVANCE → COMBINE → DATE PAIR → REPEAT WINDOW`

## Pair translation

- Card A = Tzolk'in cycle;
- Card B = Haab cycle;
- user advances time while both independently cycle;
- combined state appears only as relation metadata between the two cards;
- no binary right/wrong state;
- repeated alignment window is emergent from two periodic systems.

## Strengths

- extremely strong pair-native temporal law;
- brings R9 synchronization into the collection;
- mathematically and visually distinct from Siku temporal alternation and Ombak beat resonance;
- alternate states are inherently valid.

## Risks

# **HIGH CULTURAL-SENSITIVITY DISCIPLINE REQUIRED.**

The Smithsonian source explicitly presents Maya calendrical knowledge as living, including contemporary Day Keeper practice and ceremonial context.

Therefore:
- do not gamify sacred/calendar-keeper practice;
- do not copy glyphs merely as visual styling;
- isolate the documented cycle relationship and clearly label it as an educational structural translation;
- avoid claiming spiritual meanings not required for the mechanism;
- consider whether a living-culture family should proceed without stronger community-authored source context.

Geographic note:
- a later build must choose a defensible specific Maya geographic/context anchor rather than flattening all Maya peoples into one generic culture.

# N4 — METATE + MANO / COAHUILA / MESOAMERICAN FOOD-PREPARATION TECHNOLOGY

## Source grounding

Smithsonian National Museum of the American Indian documents a `Metate/Flat mortar and mano/grinding stone` associated with Kikapu (Mexican Kickapoo), Coahuila, Mexico.

Smithsonian National Museum of American History separately describes the mano as a cylindrical stone used horizontally against a metate and documents metates as broad concave grinding surfaces used for foods including cacao.

Sources:
- https://www.si.edu/object/metateflat-mortar-and-manogrinding-stone%3ANMAI_28704
- https://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object/nmah_867614
- https://www.si.edu/object/metate%3Anmah_867616

## Documented relationship

The mano is worked horizontally against the metate. Pressure and repeated relative movement grind material between the two stone surfaces.

## Gap-map territory

Primary: **R10 — Compression / Applied Force**
Secondary: reciprocal abrasion / transformation.

## Candidate law

# `PRESSURE PLUS RELATIVE STROKE BETWEEN STONES PRODUCES GRINDING.`

Signature:
`MANO ≠ METATE → LOAD → STROKE → ABRADE → PARTICLE SIZE CHANGES → CONTINUE`

## Pair translation

- Card A = mano / moving grinding member;
- Card B = metate / receiving grinding surface;
- direct horizontal stroke with controlled downward load;
- amount of transformation accumulates through relation rather than instant success;
- OTHER stroke/load conditions remain valid but yield different grinding progression.

## Strengths

- literal two-object source pair;
- bodily/material relation is immediately legible;
- distinct motion vocabulary from current North America;
- no decorative cultural layer required.

## Risks

- grinding-stone technologies are widespread; a later family must preserve a precise museum/community context rather than claim the mechanism as uniquely Mexican or uniquely Kikapu;
- transformation of food has cross-collection proximity to Toyama, though the primary law here is pressure/abrasion rather than reveal;
- cultural framing must not generalize one collected object into all Mesoamerican food practice.

# N5 — MÉTIS RED RIVER CART: WOOD WHEEL ↔ WOOD AXLE / PRAIRIE CANADA

## Source grounding

Strong Métis/community and museum-adjacent sources document the Red River cart as an all-wood transport system developed and used by Métis communities across the Prairies.

Sources:
- Manitoba Métis Federation: https://www.mmf.mb.ca/metis-minute/red-river-cart-2
- Indigenous Peoples Atlas of Canada: https://indigenouspeoplesatlasofcanada.ca/article/red-river-carts/
- Manitoba Museum context: https://manitobamuseum.ca/john-macoun-the-botanist-who-changed-the-map-of-western-canada/
- Manitoba Historical Society technical history: https://www.mhs.mb.ca/docs/transactions/3/redrivercart.shtml
- Smithsonian mechanical collection historical model reference: https://repository.si.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/bdccb13a-2014-459c-b095-9bea6dc75b23/content

## Documented relationship

Wooden wheels rotate on wooden axles. Sources emphasize that axles were not greased because trail dust mixed with lubricant could accelerate wear or immobilize movement. The resulting dry wood-on-wood rotation produced the cart's famous squeal. The carts' all-wood construction made repair with local materials possible.

## Gap-map territory

Primary: **R2 — Rotation / Articulation**
Secondary: friction / load-bearing material relation.

## Candidate law

# `DRY WOOD ROTATION CARRIES LOAD WHILE PRODUCING A DISTINCTIVE FRICTION SIGNATURE.`

## Pair translation

- Card A = dished wheel / hub;
- Card B = wooden axle / load path;
- pin/seat relation engages;
- wheel rotates under simulated load;
- friction/noise state emerges from material relation;
- lubrication toggle should NOT be used as a simplistic "better" state because sources explain why grease could be counterproductive on dusty trails.

## Strengths

- strong Métis specificity grounded in practical material design;
- rotation/load relation is physical and clear;
- fascinating counterintuitive constraint: lack of lubrication is not negligence but environmental adaptation;
- can be culturally specific without ornament.

## Risks

- primary rotation topology overlaps the newly promoted Astrolabe candidate;
- friction-emergence has some overlap with Hika Ahi, and sound emergence with Ombak;
- wheel + axle is mechanically common, so distinctiveness depends on the documented all-wood/dust constraint;
- may be better as a future reserve than one of only two new North America slots.

## Preliminary status

# N6 — LACROSSE STICK + BALL / MENOMINEE OBJECT ANCHOR, WISCONSIN

## Source grounding

Smithsonian National Museum of the American Indian holds a documented Menominee lacrosse stick-and-ball set collected in Wisconsin in 1919, as well as numerous Nation-specific historic lacrosse sticks.

Primary object source:
- https://americanindian.si.edu/collections-search/object/NMAI_99145

Corroborating collection examples:
- Huron-Wendat stick, Canadian Museum of History: https://www.historymuseum.ca/collections/artifact/46866
- Tuscarora, Seneca, Kickapoo and other NMAI sticks in NMAI collections.

## Documented relationship

The primary source itself preserves the stick and ball as a set. The laced/bent pocket of the stick receives and controls the ball through capture, cradle and release during play.

## Gap-map territory

Primary: **R5 — Capture / Temporary Engagement**
Secondary: **R10 — Stored/tensioned control through pocket geometry**

## Candidate law

Working law:
# `A LACED POCKET TURNS BALL CONTACT INTO TEMPORARY CONTROL.`

Signature:
`BALL ≠ POCKET → CONTACT → RECEIVE → CRADLE → RETAIN / RELEASE`

## Pair translation

- Card A = ball;
- Card B = documented stick/pocket;
- approach/contact can become capture rather than permanent lock;
- cradle motion demonstrates controlled retention;
- release restores separation;
- no success/failure scoring needed.

## Strengths

- source is literally a two-object set;
- introduces temporary capture/release instead of permanent latch;
- dynamic and visually immediate;
- historic Nation-specific museum anchor is available.

## Risks

# **CULTURAL CONTEXT MUST NOT BE FLATTENED.**

Lacrosse has distinct histories and meanings among multiple Indigenous Nations and remains a living practice/sport.

Therefore:
- do not present a generic pan-Indigenous "lacrosse culture";
- choose one defensible object/community anchor if promoted;
- do not invent ceremonial or spiritual meaning;
- avoid turning the family into a sports mini-game;
- relation must remain material capture/control, not points/scoring.

## Preliminary status
