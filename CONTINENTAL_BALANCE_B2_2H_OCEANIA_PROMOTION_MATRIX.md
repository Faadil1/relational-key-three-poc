# FINALIST O1 — TONGIAKI DOUBLE-HULLED CANOE / TONGA

## Deep-source evidence

Te Ara states that two hulls gave Polynesian vessels stability and seaworthiness and documents a double-hulled canoe observed at Tongatapu in 1643.

Te Ara separately describes Tongan `tongiaki` as more stable than nearby fishing outriggers.

Te Papa preserves a commissioned model of an eighteenth-century Tongan tongiaki with two equal-length hulls joined by a deck, and its Pacific canoe documentation describes double hulls as two hulls joined with booms and decking.

Sources:
- https://teara.govt.nz/en/artwork/5987/polynesian-double-hull
- https://teara.govt.nz/en/artwork/2213/tongiaki-in-tongatapu-tonga
- https://collections.tepapa.govt.nz/object/648909
- https://collections.tepapa.govt.nz/topic/2354

# FINALIST O6 — GARAMUT + WOODEN POUNDER / EAST SEPIK-RAMU, PAPUA NEW GUINEA

## Deep-source evidence

National Gallery of Australia documentation for an East Sepik / Ramu River garamut states that the drum is pounded with blunt sticks and that complex rhythms function as communicative, codified language capable of transmitting information over long distance.

The Fralin Museum of Art describes garamut communication as rhythms and tones beaten on the resonant body with a wooden pounder to announce meetings, issue warnings and communicate with neighboring villages. The exhibition was developed with consultation including Andrew Moutu, former director of Papua New Guinea’s National Museum & Art Gallery.

An indigenous-knowledge paper from the University of Papua New Guinea documents garamut signals in Maramba society as a traditional communication medium.

Sources:
- https://digital.nga.gov.au/archive/exhibition/mythmagic/default.cfm?irn=211455&bioartistirn=211455&mystartrow=61&realstartrow=61&mnuid=3&galid=0&viewid=2
- https://uvafralinartmuseum.virginia.edu/exhibitions/voices-connection-garamut-slit-drums-new-guinea
- https://pngbuai.com/600technology/information/waigani/drums/WS97-sec7-Norman2.html

## Primary relational identity

# **R11 — ENCODE / SIGNAL THROUGH ORDERED IMPACT**

Secondary:
contact/impact.

## Candidate law

# **ORDERED IMPACTS ON A GARAMUT TURN RHYTHM INTO A LONG-DISTANCE SIGNAL.**

Signature:
`POUNDER ≠ GARAMUT → STRIKE → ORDER → SIGNAL → CARRY`

## Pair proof

- the pounder and resonant body are distinct physical members;
- direct impact creates sound;
- ordered timing converts isolated impacts into a signal sequence;
- the emergent proof can remain a minimal timing trace rather than a third dashboard;
- the build can demonstrate encoding as a structural principle without reproducing an authentic restricted message.

## Sound-family comparison

Ombak:
`SIMULTANEOUS NEAR-UNISON DIFFERENCE → BEATING / RESONANCE`

Siku:
`DISTRIBUTED NOTES → ALTERNATION → ONE MELODY`

Garamut:
`ORDERED IMPACT EVENTS → SIGNAL SEQUENCE / COMMUNICATION`

The medium overlaps sound, but the information law does not.

## Cultural safeguarding boundary

# **NO AUTHENTIC CODE SIMULATION CLAIM.**

The build must not:
- label an invented pulse sequence as a real warning, death notice, invitation or personal-name signal;
- teach community-specific signaling rules;
- reproduce restricted ceremonial knowledge;
- copy clan/spirit carvings as decorative UI styling;
- imply one PNG-wide universal garamut code.

The safest translation is:
`EDITORIAL SIGNAL SEQUENCE · TIMING DEMONSTRATION ONLY · NOT COMMUNITY CODE`.

A spec must choose one defensible regional source anchor and keep broader variation explicit.

Potential `OTHER SEQUENCE`:

- result language states only `EDITORIAL SIGNAL PATTERN NOT COMPLETED`;
- no cultural message is declared invalid.

# FINALIST O4 — KULA SOULAVA + MWALI / MASSIM REGION, PAPUA NEW GUINEA

## Deep-source evidence

The Metropolitan Museum of Art describes Kula as a reciprocal exchange cycle in which soulava / bagi necklaces and mwali armbands move in opposite directions through pre-established paths, sustaining long-lived relations among exchange partners.

Sources:
- https://www.metmuseum.org/exhibitions/arts-of-oceania/inside-the-exhibition
- https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/311225
- https://collections.bowers.org/objects/138373/shell-currency-mwali
- https://collections.bowers.org/objects/141010/necklace-currency-soulava-or-bagi

## Primary relational identity

# **R8 — RECIPROCAL COUNTER-CIRCULATION**

## Candidate law

# **OPPOSITE CIRCULATION OF SOULAVA AND MWALI SUSTAINS A RECIPROCAL EXCHANGE NETWORK.**

Mechanically and conceptually this is exceptional.

## Why it is NOT promoted now

The strongest causal unit is not simply `object A + object B`. It is a distributed living network of partners, journeys, histories, obligations and reciprocal relations across time.

A two-card prototype risks making the most extractive possible simplification:
`red necklace token ↻ + white armband token ↺ = attractive diagram`.

That would preserve the geometry while losing the actual social relationship.

The rarity of R8 does not justify that reduction while other high-quality lower-risk candidates exist.

## Decision

# **DO NOT PROMOTE IN CURRENT ROUND / CONTEXT-SENSITIVITY HOLD**

This is a safeguarding decision, not a quality rejection.

Future reconsideration would require materially stronger community-led framing and a product architecture capable of representing partnership/history rather than merely object circulation.
