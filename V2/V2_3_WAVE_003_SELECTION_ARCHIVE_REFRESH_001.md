# 1 — BOULLE · FRANCE

Slug: `boulle-france`

## Refreshed authoritative basis

Primary mechanism anchor:

The Metropolitan Museum of Art — Small desk with folding top (`bureau brisé`), ca. 1685:

- https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/207667

The current Met object record explicitly describes the relevant structural technique:

- a thin sheet of tortoiseshell and a thin sheet of brass were placed together;
- the design was cut through both layers;
- the layers were then separated;
- one surface uses the light material in the dark ground (`première partie`);
- the reciprocal surface uses the leftover inverse materials (`contre partie`);
- the resulting pair is an exact material reverse.

The Met marks the referenced object image **Public Domain** and provides Open Access resources.

Structural corroboration:

The Met — Wig cabinet / related marquetry technique:

- https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/231057

The record likewise describes tortoiseshell and metal sheets stacked and cut together, then assembled as light-on-dark and dark-on-light reciprocal patterns.

## V1 inheritance

Frozen V1 identifies the strongest mechanism as:

`SHARED CUT → RECIPROCAL INVERSION`

V2 must preserve this as a pair-owned material consequence, not turn the edition into a decorative Boulle-pattern viewer.

## V2 pair contract

**Card A — MATERIAL STACK / PREMIÈRE-PARTIE SIDE**

Owns one material layer and its post-cut surface.

**Relation — ONE SHARED CUT THROUGH BOTH LAYERS**

Owns only the cut path and separation/recombination event.

**Card B — RECIPROCAL MATERIAL / CONTRE-PARTIE SIDE**

Owns the inverse layer and complementary post-cut surface.

Canonical law:

`STACKED MATERIAL A + B → ONE SHARED CUT → SEPARATE → TWO RECIPROCAL INVERSE SURFACES`

## Memorable moment

One bounded cut is applied only once, but separating the two members reveals two opposite valid outcomes simultaneously. Removing either member destroys the reciprocity.

## Alternate-valid semantics

Do not make one side correct and the other a failure.

Preferred states:

- `STACKED`
- `SHARED CUT REGISTERED`
- `SEPARATED · PREMIÈRE / CONTRE PARTIE`
- `OTHER CUT · RECIPROCAL PAIR STILL VALID`

## Truth boundary

- demonstrate the stacked-cut / reciprocal-material principle only;
- do not claim Boulle invented the technique;
- do not reproduce a protected or unnecessary ornamental composition;
- no runtime freehand CSG is required to prove the relation;
- use a bounded procedural cut geometry whose reciprocity is deterministic.

## Asset / rights decision

`PROCEDURAL_GEOMETRY_DEFAULT__MET_PUBLIC_DOMAIN_REFERENCE_OPTIONAL_IF_MATERIALLY_NEEDED`

The selected Met object is Public Domain, but archive imagery should still be used only if it materially improves evaluator understanding. The mechanism can and should remain legible through procedural layers.

## Runtime target

MEDIUM.

Prefer pre-authored / deterministic paired cut geometry over unrestricted runtime CSG. Idle render should be demand-driven where practical.

## Collision verdict

Nearest current candidates:

- Kento — alignment + pressure → transferred register;
- Coupler — contact → latch/load;
- Funicular — reciprocal motion.

**PASS because the consequence is unique:**

`ONE SHARED CUT → TWO SIMULTANEOUS RECIPROCAL MATERIAL OUTCOMES`

No current V2 promotion candidate owns this memorable moment.
