# SOURCE BASIS

## The Metropolitan Museum of Art
Source:
`https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/320679`

Documents:
- kente wrappers are assembled from long narrow loom-woven cloth cut into bands and sewn together;
- plain weave consists of warp and weft;
- warp yarns are held under tension on the loom and interlaced by the weft.

Additional Met source:
`https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/894795`

Documents:
- Asante kente woven on a horizontal double-heddle loom;
- loom produces narrow bands roughly four inches wide;
- finished cloth is assembled from many narrow strips sewn together edge-to-edge.

## Victoria and Albert Museum
Source:
`https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/cloth-of-a-continent-africa-fashion/`

Documents:
- kente is formed by stitching narrow strips of handwoven cloth together;
- Asante and Ewe weavers traditionally use a small double-heddle handloom;
- strips are warp-striped and can include weft-float patterning;
- multiple strips create the finished cloth.

## British Museum
Source:
`https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/E_Af2004-04-1`

Documents:
- Asante kente garment composed of 24 narrow strips;
- strips joined at the selvedges by hand-sewn overcast stitch;
- plain weave and supplementary weft documented.

Bonwire object source:
`https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/E_Af1981-26-1`

Documents:
- single narrow Asante kente strip;
- found/acquired in Bonwire;
- warp colours run lengthwise through the strip;
- woven strip remains a complete valid textile object before later assembly.

## Smithsonian National Museum of African Art
