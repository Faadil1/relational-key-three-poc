## Why Bonwire
Bonwire provides a specific place-based textile context with strong documentation of Asante kente weaving, real loom process imagery and reusable Creative Commons photography. This avoids treating kente as a generic Ghanaian or pan-African visual shorthand.

## Source-grounded construction law
The Metropolitan Museum of Art documents Asante kente as narrow bands woven on a horizontal double-heddle loom and then assembled edge-to-edge into a finished cloth. This makes strip continuity a real construction property, not an invented interaction metaphor.

## Card grammar
The CR80 credential remains unmistakable at all times.

### Surface architecture
- 55–70% of the front is real photographed textile or weaving detail.
- One or two narrow strip boundaries remain visible as the relational structure.
- Metadata is placed on a quieter dark/neutral field and must not imitate a traditional textile pattern.
- The back uses provenance, place, medium and source information rather than a fake pattern.

### Interaction
STRIP → ALIGN → INTERLACE → REGISTER → JOIN → CONTINUE

#### Dormant
Two woven strips approach a narrow discontinuity inside the card. The break is small and materially plausible.

#### Align
One strip shifts by only a few pixels so selvage/edge logic approaches registration.

#### Interlace
Warp/weft direction becomes visible at the seam through subtle motion or masking. No external icon appears.

#### Register
The break collapses; the textile surface becomes continuous.

#### Join
The seam remains perceptible only as a real stitched/selvage transition, not as an oversized junction.

#### Continue
A RELATIONAL KEY continuation path can become visible only after the textile continuity resolves.

### No-match
The strips remain valid textile fields but their edge registration stays offset. No red X, no rejection theatrics.

## Visual law
