# E1 — CATOPTRIC CYLINDER ANAMORPHOSIS / FRANCE + EARLY-MODERN EUROPE

## Source grounding

Science Museum Group preserves:
- a cylindrical mirror explicitly described as `for viewing anamorphosis paintings`;
- a pre-1753 catoptric cylinder with fourteen coloured pictures.

The Metropolitan Museum of Art documents Jean François Niceron’s seventeenth-century French work in catoptric anamorphosis and an engraving in which an unreadable/distorted drawing becomes an elephant in the reflection of a cylindrical mirror.

The Rijksmuseum holds Niceron’s `La perspective curieuse`, whose Book III treats anamorphosis using reflections on cylindrical and conical surfaces.

Sources:
- https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co8023088/cylindrical-mirror
- https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co2043/anamorphic-picture
- https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/393576
- https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/405839
- https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/nl/collectie/publicatie/La-perspective-curieuse-du-reverend-P-Niceron-minime-divisee-en-quatre-livres-avec-loptique-et-la-catoptrique-Mersenne-du-mesme-ordre-mise-en-lumiere-apres-la-mort-de-lautheur-oeuvre-tres-utile-aux-peintres-architectes-sculpteurs-graveurs-et-a-tous-autres-qui-se-meslent-du-dessein--882b0c66978e15f2a0fea49f1053d696

## Documented relationship

The image is intentionally distorted in ordinary view. A cylindrical mirror placed in the intended relationship re-maps the reflected geometry into a legible image.

## Gap-map territory

Primary: **R6 — Reflection / Optical Decode**
Secondary: encode/decode.

## Candidate law

# **CURVED REFLECTION TURNS A DISTORTED IMAGE INTO A LEGIBLE FORM.**

Signature:
`DISTORTED IMAGE ≠ CYLINDER MIRROR → POSITION → REFLECT → GEOMETRY RE-MAPS → IMAGE READS`

## Pair translation

- Card A = `ANAMORPHIC IMAGE FIELD`;
- Card B = `CYLINDRICAL MIRROR`;
- direct movement of the cylinder changes whether the reflected image resolves;
- reflected proof should remain inside/adjacent to Card B, not become a third hero screen;
- source-derived or public-domain anamorphic material can be used rather than inventing a supposedly historical image.

## Strengths

- extremely strong two-object relation;
- reflection/decode is absent from the collection;
- dramatic visual proof with low interaction ambiguity;
- public-domain early-modern visual anchors exist;
- cultural identity comes from a documented optical practice/treatise rather than ornament.

## Risks

- Europe already contains Stereoscopy, so the family must read as `distortion → curved reflection → rectification`, never binocular depth/fusion;
- mirror may feel receiver-like unless both cards retain strong bounded identity;
- exact optical reconstruction requires careful geometry; an editorial approximation must be labeled as such unless mathematically verified.

# E2 — STRAIGHT SLIDE RULE / LONDON, UNITED KINGDOM

## Source grounding

Science Museum Group preserves Robert Bissaker’s 1654 slide rule from London, identified as the earliest-known dated straight slide rule.

The museum states that William Oughtred first used logarithmic scales to form a calculating instrument with scales that could move relative to one another.

Sources:
- https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co60116/slide-rule-by-robert-bissaker-slide-rule
- https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co60474/thompsons-slide-rule

## Documented relationship

Two logarithmic scale fields are translated relative to one another. Their alignment lets multiplicative/divisive relationships be read through addition/subtraction of logarithmic distances.

## Gap-map territory

Primary: **R11 — Encoded Scale / Compute by Relative Translation**
Secondary: linear articulation.

## Candidate law

# **RELATIVE DISPLACEMENT OF LOGARITHMIC SCALES TURNS DISTANCE ADDITION INTO MULTIPLICATIVE READING.**

Signature:
`FIXED SCALE ≠ SLIDING SCALE → ALIGN INDEX → TRANSLATE → READ PRODUCT / RATIO`

## Pair translation

- Card A = fixed logarithmic scale;
- Card B = sliding logarithmic scale;
- user directly drags one scale;
- selected editorial input pair produces a read point without calculator/dashboard dominance;
- OTHER alignment remains another valid calculation state.

## Strengths

- literal paired-scale interaction;
- no third physical object necessary for the core relation;
- historical object anchor is unusually precise;
- computation emerges through relation, not one card alone.

## Risks

- relative motion has visual/system overlap with Astrolabe even though linear translation differs from rotation;
- Europe already has a UK family;
- exact logarithmic reading should either be mathematically correct or explicitly labeled a structural demonstration;
- could become a calculator UI rather than a relational object.

# E3 — CYLINDER MUSIC BOX: PINNED CYLINDER + TUNED COMB / SWISS JURA

## Source grounding

Switzerland’s federal Museum of Music Automatons preserves internationally important collections of Swiss cylinder music boxes and more than 12,000 music media including pin-studded wooden/metal cylinders. The museum frames this craft as a major Swiss industrial/export tradition originating in the Jura arc.

Smithsonian examples from St. Croix, Switzerland document cylinder music boxes with one-piece tuned combs.

Museo Nicolis describes the operating principle: pins on a revolving cylinder/disc pluck tuned teeth/lamellae of a steel comb to produce musical notes.

Sources:
- https://www.musikautomaten.ch/en/collection-en
- https://www.musikautomaten.ch/en/the-collections
- https://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object/nmah_606279
- https://www.si.edu/object/paillard-cie-cylinder-music-box%3Anmah_605759
- https://www.museonicolis.com/en/cylinder-sound-box/

## Documented relationship

Pin placement on the rotating cylinder stores event geometry. As the cylinder turns, pins engage tuned comb teeth in a specific order, converting stored spatial pattern into ordered notes.

## Gap-map territory

Primary: **R11 — Mechanical Encode / Decode**
Secondary: R9 sequence/time.

## Candidate law

# **PIN GEOMETRY ON A ROTATING CYLINDER BECOMES ORDERED NOTES WHEN IT ENGAGES A TUNED COMB.**

Signature:
`PINNED CYLINDER ≠ TUNED COMB → ROTATE → PLUCK → ORDER → PHRASE`

## Pair translation

- Card A = `PINNED CYLINDER / STORED SEQUENCE`;
- Card B = `TUNED COMB / PHYSICAL DECODER`;
- user directly rotates the cylinder;
- a short illustrative pin pattern activates only corresponding comb teeth;
- audio must be synthetic/editorial unless an authenticated recording is sourced.

## Strengths

- exceptionally legible stored-information → action relation;
- strong Swiss/Jura industrial-cultural anchor;
- physical contact directly links the two members;
- unlike Garamut, information is spatially stored before performance rather than authored as timing during performance.

## Risks

- sound medium is crowded by Ombak, Siku and Garamut;
- rotating-cylinder visual motion overlaps Astrolabe at a generic level;
- spring/winding drive is a third mechanical subsystem in historical boxes, so prototype should use direct manual rotation and clearly isolate cylinder↔comb;
- do not claim synthetic notes reproduce a historic tune.

# E4 — PANTOGRAPH / DILLINGEN, GERMANY — SCHEINER 1603

## Source grounding

The City Museum / history portal of Ingolstadt documents Christoph Scheiner’s pantograph invention in 1603 during his stay in Dillingen and his 1631 `Pantographice` publication.

Smithsonian NMAH describes the pantograph as a drawing instrument used to enlarge/reduce figures and identifies Scheiner’s 1603 invention. Museo Galileo’s historical invention archive describes the four-bar parallelogram, fixed centre, tracing point and writing point and explains how their relative positions produce enlarged, reduced or same-scale copies.

Sources:
- https://www.ingolstadt.de/stadtmuseum/scheuerer/ausstell/schein11.htm
- https://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object-groups/pantographs
- https://redi.imss.fi.it/inventions/index.php/Pantograph
- https://www.si.edu/object/pantograph%3Anmah_1294148

## Documented relationship

A tracing point and drawing point are constrained by a parallelogram linkage so that movement of the tracer produces a proportional corresponding movement of the writer.

## Gap-map territory

Primary: **Proportional Transformation / Coupled Motion**
Secondary: R4 linkage / R8 role correspondence.

## Candidate law

# **A LINKED PARALLELOGRAM TURNS ONE TRACED MOTION INTO A SCALED COPY AT A SECOND POINT.**

Signature:
`TRACER ≠ WRITER → LINK → TRACE → PROPORTIONAL MOTION → SCALED COPY`

## Pair translation

- Card A = `TRACER POINT / SOURCE MOTION`;
- Card B = `WRITING POINT / RESULT MOTION`;
- direct user drag on tracer drives writer proportionally;
- optional role reversal may demonstrate enlarge/reduce configuration;
- linkage must remain a thin subordinate relation field.

## Strengths

- new proportional-transformation law;
- direct input on one member produces constrained output at the other;
- strong Central European historical source trail;
- visually and mechanically distinct from current families.

## Risks

# **CENTER/LINKAGE PRODUCT RISK.**

The parallelogram rods are not incidental: they are the actual mechanism. If the linkage becomes visually dominant, the center becomes the product and the family fails the invariant.

Also, the drawing itself is an output field and must stay subordinate.

# E5 — ANCHOR ESCAPEMENT / ENGLISH CLOCKMAKING

## Source grounding

Science Museum Group preserves multiple anchor escapement models and seventeenth/eighteenth-century longcase clock movements using anchor escapements.

British Horological Institute training material describes the escape wheel teeth engaging pallets; pendulum-linked pallet movement releases an escape-wheel tooth and gives impulse to sustain oscillation.

The Met describes early anchor escapements as alternately giving impulse to and locking the escape wheel.

Sources:
- https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co745/model-of-anchor-escapement
- https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/search/objects/object_type/anchor-escapement
- https://bhi.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/BHI-DLC-Tech-L1.pdf
- https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/203672

## Documented relationship

The escape wheel and anchor/pallet system alternately lock and release. Through the pendulum-linked oscillation, continuous stored drive is converted into regulated incremental wheel motion.

## Gap-map territory

Primary: **R9 — Regulation / Alternating Lock-Release**
Secondary: latch/contact.

## Candidate law

# **ALTERNATING PALLET LOCK AND RELEASE TURNS CONTINUOUS DRIVE INTO REGULATED STEPS.**

Signature:
`ESCAPE WHEEL ≠ ANCHOR/PALLETS → LOCK → SWING → RELEASE → STEP → LOCK OTHER SIDE`

## Pair translation

Potential:
- Card A = `ESCAPE WHEEL`;
- Card B = `ANCHOR + PENDULUM ASSEMBLY`;
- direct oscillatory drag on Card B yields one step at Card A;
- no autonomous clock simulation required.

## Strengths

- strong time-regulation relation;
- stepwise output differs from Valparaíso counterweight and Janney one-time latch;
- museum source corpus is deep.

## Risks

# **PAIR-REDUCTION RISK.**

A functioning clock escapement depends on drive energy and the pendulum/oscillator; reducing the mechanism to only wheel + anchor can misstate causality unless Card B is explicitly an anchor+pendulum assembly and the drive is isolated as external context.

UK geography also duplicates Stereoscopy and Slide Rule.

# E6 — CARDAN GRILLE / ITALIAN RENAISSANCE STEGANOGRAPHY

## Source grounding

National Cryptologic Foundation historical material describes the Cardan/Jerome Cardan grille as a card perforated with holes at designated points, laid over a page of text so that only letters visible through the holes are read.

Historical attribution is to Gerolamo/Girolamo Cardano, the sixteenth-century Italian polymath.

Source:
- https://cryptologicfoundation.org/file_download/inline/a1032994-6c51-4114-82a9-93ab73e29673

## Documented relationship

A perforated mask selects only specific positions from a larger text field. Meaningful content becomes visible through occlusion rather than through changing the underlying page.

## Gap-map territory

Primary: **R6 — Occlusion / Selective Visibility**
Secondary: R11 decode.

## Candidate law

# **A PERFORATED GRILLE TURNS A FULL TEXT FIELD INTO A SELECTIVE READING.**

Signature:
`TEXT PAGE ≠ GRILLE → ALIGN → OCCLUDE → SELECT → READ`

## Pair translation

- Card A = `TEXT FIELD`;
- Card B = `PERFORATED GRILLE`;
- direct movement/alignment of grille selects visible positions;
- OTHER alignment reveals a different subset without declaring either member invalid.

## Strengths

- nearly perfect two-layer physical pair;
- opens selective visibility / occlusion;
- high interaction clarity;
- no central third product is necessary.

## Risks

# E7 — JACQUARD PUNCHED CARD + READER / LYON-FRANCE OR EUROPEAN LOOM CONTEXT

## Source grounding

Science Museum Group documents Jacquard cards and a working Jacquard hand loom:
- pattern instructions are stored in holes punched in cards;
- cards are forced against pins;
- pins pass through holes and stop where there are no holes;
- this controls which warp threads are raised and therefore the woven pattern.

The museum notes Joseph Jacquard’s French development of the mechanism in 1804–1805.

Sources:
- https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co471243/jacquard-cards-jacquard-cards
- https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co8405056/jacquard-hand-loom
- https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co44902

## Documented relationship

The punched card is a physical instruction medium. A pin/needle reader responds differently to holes versus solid areas, converting stored pattern into selected warp lifting.

## Gap-map territory

Primary: **R11 — Physical Encode / Decode**
Secondary: textile selection.

## Candidate law

# **PUNCHED ABSENCE IN A CARD CONTROLS WHICH LOOM PINS PASS AND WHICH THREADS RISE.**

Signature:
`PUNCHED CARD ≠ PIN READER → PRESS → HOLE/PRESENCE TEST → SELECT WARP → PATTERN`

## Pair translation

Potential:
- Card A = `PUNCHED INSTRUCTION CARD`;
- Card B = `PIN / NEEDLE READER FIELD`;
- direct card approach produces a binary physical readout;
- woven output remains tiny proof metadata, not the product.

## Strengths

- powerful material encoding law;
- excellent institutional source;
- holes as information produce a distinct physical relationship;
- connection to early information storage is historically strong.

## Risks

- actual Jacquard system includes cards, cylinder/reader, hooks, warp threads and loom;
- pair reduction can easily become dishonest;
- textile output overlaps Bonwire at medium level;
- France already has Boulle;
- R11 is now represented by Garamut and potentially Music Box.

## Preliminary status

# **STRONG SOURCE / SYSTEM-REDUCTION RESERVE**
