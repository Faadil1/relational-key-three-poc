## Archive mechanism
Japanese polychrome woodblock printing uses separate carved blocks for different colours and depends on registration guides so repeated impressions land precisely on the same sheet.

The British Museum describes `kentō` registration using two cut notches: a corner guide (`kagi`) and a long-edge guide (`hikitsuki`). The paper is positioned against these guides for each colour separation.

The Metropolitan Museum of Art notes that polychrome ukiyo-e used separate blocks for each colour, sometimes many blocks, and alignment cuts at the edge of the blocks to maintain precision across repeated impressions.
