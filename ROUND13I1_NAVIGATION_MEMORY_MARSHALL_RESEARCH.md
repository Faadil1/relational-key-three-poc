## Continental Diversity Gate

## Source-grounded mechanism
Marshallese stick charts / navigational charts are documented as abstract teaching and memory devices that encode relationships among ocean swell patterns, islands/atolls, currents and directional cues.

Supported claims:
- Smithsonian NMNH describes curved sticks as showing where swells are deflected by an island, short straight strips as currents near islands, longer strips as directional indications, and cowrie shells as islands.
- Smithsonian Ocean distinguishes training/teaching functions from real-time map use and describes swell reflection/refraction and confused seas as relevant observed phenomena.
- The Met describes rebbilib as stylized but functional objects carrying information about island locations and wave patterns; curved strips may represent ocean swells.

## Truthfulness boundary
This prototype does NOT:
- decode a specific historical chart;
- assign exact island identities to marks in the visual;
- claim the chart is to scale;
- present the object as a modern GPS-style route map;
- claim every stick or shell has one universal meaning independent of maker/context.
