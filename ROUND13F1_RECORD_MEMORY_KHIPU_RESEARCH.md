## Source-grounded facts

### Smithsonian Institution
The Smithsonian describes quipu/khipu as recording devices used by the Inka Empire, generally made from colored, spun and plied threads/strings. Historic documentation indicates record keeping and message carrying. The Smithsonian specifically notes that color, knot placement and cord type all participate in the logical numerical reading of a quipu.

Source:
https://www.si.edu/collections/snapshot/quipu

NMAI object 14/3866 is catalogued as a khipu / counting record, probably Inka, Peru, AD 1450–1532, cotton cordage and dyes, tied/knotted.

Source:
https://collections.si.edu/search/detail/edanmdm:NMAI_154591

### Khipu Field Guide
The Field Guide documents a structural vocabulary including:
- primary cords,
- pendant cords,
- subsidiary cord clusters,
- cord attachments,
- twist direction,
- cord colors,
- knots.

Source:
https://www.khipufieldguide.com/sketchbook/Khipu_Legend.html
