# O1 — TONGIAKI DOUBLE-HULLED CANOE / TONGA

## Source grounding

Te Ara documents a Polynesian double hull observed at Tongatapu in 1643 and states that two hulls gave stability and seaworthiness.

Te Papa documents an eighteenth-century Tongan `tongiaki` model with **two hulls of equal length joined together by a deck**, and its Pacific canoe documentation explains that double-hulled canoes join two hulls with booms and decking.

Sources:
- https://teara.govt.nz/en/artwork/5987/polynesian-double-hull
- https://teara.govt.nz/en/artwork/2213/tongiaki-in-tongatapu-tonga
- https://collections.tepapa.govt.nz/object/648909
- https://collections.tepapa.govt.nz/topic/2354

## Documented relationship

Two bounded hulls are held in parallel and structurally joined. Their coupled geometry creates a broader distributed buoyancy platform associated with greater stability than a single narrow hull.

## Gap-map territory

Primary: **R4 — Structural Coupling / Load Distribution**
Secondary: **R1 — Balance / Counter-support**

## Candidate law

# **LASHED PARALLEL HULLS DISTRIBUTE BUOYANCY INTO A MORE STABLE PLATFORM.**

Signature:
`HULL A ≠ HULL B → PARALLEL → JOIN → DISTRIBUTE → STABILIZE`

## Pair translation

- Card A = `HULL A`;
- Card B = `HULL B`;
- approach establishes parallel spacing;
- the relationship field may show booms/deck connection only as subordinate structural evidence;
- a restrained lean/load cue demonstrates that stability is a property of the coupled system;
- both hulls remain visibly primary before and after coupling.

## Strengths

- literal two-body structural system;
- strong source trail with a specific Tongan anchor;
- immediate physical legibility;
- mechanism is materially distinct from Hika Ahi;
- geographic addition would broaden Oceania beyond Marshall Islands + Aotearoa.

## Risks

- maritime context can feel too close to Swell if navigation, stars, waves or route-finding become the hero;
- water is an external environmental condition, so the build must claim structural stability rather than simulate naval engineering;
- booms/deck must not become a third product.

# O2 — I‘E TAPA BEATER + WOODEN ANVIL / SĀMOA

## Source grounding

Te Papa documents a Sāmoan `i‘e` tapa beater and describes barkcloth making: prepared inner bark is pounded with the i‘e on a **special wooden anvil until it widens into a larger size**.

Sources:
- https://collections.tepapa.govt.nz/object/148134
- https://www.tepapa.govt.nz/digital-museum/explore-digital-museum/tapa-barkcloth-art-in-the-pacific

## Documented relationship

A wooden beater repeatedly impacts bark supported on a wooden anvil. Repeated compression flattens and widens the bark strip into a larger barkcloth surface.

## Gap-map territory

Primary: **R10 — Impact / Compression / Material Spread**
Secondary: accumulation through repeated work.

## Candidate law

# **REPEATED IMPACT BETWEEN BEATER AND ANVIL WIDENS BARK INTO CLOTH.**

Signature:
`I‘E ≠ ANVIL → PLACE BARK → IMPACT → COMPRESS → WIDEN → CONTINUE`

## Pair translation

- Card A = `I‘E / BEATER`;
- Card B = `WOODEN ANVIL / SUPPORT`;
- bark is a subordinate material field, not a third card;
- direct repeated strikes progressively widen the represented strip;
- no single strike should resolve the family.

## Strengths

- genuine tool relationship;
- tactile interaction and visible material transformation;
- Sāmoan source anchor is precise;
- distinct from Kento if no pattern transfer is shown.

## Risks

- bark is causally necessary, so the system is triadic even if the product pair is beater + anvil;
- R10 is now occupied by Metate, although the motion law differs: impact/compression versus pressure/reciprocal abrasion;
- do not mix Samoan, Tongan, Tahitian or Fijian terminology into one generic “tapa” family;
- decorative tapa patterns must not become the mechanism.

## Preliminary status

# O3 — TOKI BLADE + HAFT SYSTEM / TOKELAU

## Source grounding

Te Papa documents Tokelauan toki as three-part tools:
- `kau toki` — handle;
- `lau toki` — blade;
- `lau kafa` — lashing.

A Nukunonu example has a tridacna-shell blade lashed to a wooden handle with plaited sennit in an X pattern. Te Papa also documents continuing Tokelauan toki-making under tufuga Fuli Fati, including steel blades lashed to handles with sennit.

Sources:
- https://collections.tepapa.govt.nz/object/91479
- https://collections.tepapa.govt.nz/object/450080
- https://collections.tepapa.govt.nz/object/450081

## Documented relationship

A short blade becomes a handled adze when seated and tightly secured to the wooden haft. The lashing transmits motion/load between blade and handle.

## Gap-map territory

Primary: **R4 — Structural Load Transfer / Hafting**
Secondary: R5 engagement.

## Candidate law

# **HAFTING TURNS A SEPARATE BLADE AND HANDLE INTO ONE CONTROLLED LOAD PATH.**

Signature:
`BLADE ≠ HANDLE → SEAT → LASH → LOAD PATH → LEVER → WORK`

## Pair translation

Potential:
- Card A = `LAU TOKI / BLADE`;
- Card B = `KAU TOKI / HANDLE RECEIVER`;
- lashing appears only as subordinate relation evidence during engagement.

## Strengths

- very strong Tokelauan object documentation;
- continuing cultural-practice source context rather than generic “Pacific adze” framing;
- clear structural transformation from separate components to working composite;
- visual identity can come entirely from real tool geometry/materials.

## Risks

# **THREE-PART TRUTHFULNESS PROBLEM.**

Te Papa explicitly defines the lashing as a third named part of the toki. A two-card build must not erase that fact or pretend blade + handle alone are sufficient.

Therefore this candidate is only viable if the relationship field can truthfully represent the `lau kafa` as a connector without making it the product.

Broad action language also has some overlap with Janney (`engage → load path`) even though the mechanics differ substantially.

## Preliminary status

# O4 — KULA: SOULAVA + MWALI COUNTER-CIRCULATION / MASSIM REGION, PAPUA NEW GUINEA

## Source grounding

The Metropolitan Museum of Art documents the Massim Kula cycle as a reciprocal exchange network in which:
- `soulava` / bagi red-shell necklaces circulate clockwise;
- `mwali` shell armbands circulate counterclockwise;
- the cycle sustains long-lived relationships among exchange partners.

Bowers Museum independently documents the same opposing circulation of the two valuables.

Sources:
- https://www.metmuseum.org/exhibitions/arts-of-oceania/inside-the-exhibition
- https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/311225
- https://collections.bowers.org/objects/138373/shell-currency-mwali
- https://collections.bowers.org/objects/141010/necklace-currency-soulava-or-bagi

## Documented relationship

Two distinct classes of valuables move continuously in opposite directions through a regional network of partners. Their counter-circulation is linked to reciprocal exchange relationships rather than a one-time direct swap.

## Gap-map territory

Primary: **R8 — Exchange / Counter-Circulation / Reciprocity**
Secondary: R9 cyclic continuation.

## Candidate law

# **OPPOSITE CIRCULATION OF SOULAVA AND MWALI SUSTAINS A RECIPROCAL EXCHANGE NETWORK.**

Signature:
`SOULAVA ↻ / MWALI ↺ → PASS → RECIPROCATE → CIRCULATE → RELATION CONTINUES`

## Pair translation

- Card A = `SOULAVA / CLOCKWISE VALUABLE`;
- Card B = `MWALI / COUNTERCLOCKWISE VALUABLE`;
- neither object approaches the other for a simplistic instant trade;
- movement should remain opposite and distributed across an editorial network arc;
- history/circulation may accumulate without implying private ownership as the core mechanic.

## Strengths

- extraordinarily rare R8 relationship territory;
- the two object classes are source-documented and materially distinct;
- opposite movement creates immediate visual difference;
- mechanism is unlike every current RELATIONAL KEY family.

## Risks

# **HIGH CONTEXT-SENSITIVITY / SOCIAL-SYSTEM REDUCTION RISK.**

Kula is an ongoing ceremonial/social exchange system, not a mechanical toy. A prototype could easily flatten life-long partnerships, status, travel and community relations into two tokens orbiting a diagram.

The two valuables are also not necessarily exchanged for one another simultaneously at a single encounter; the build must preserve counter-circulation rather than invent direct barter.

No generic “tribal”, canoe, shell or map ornament should be added beyond source-grounded needs.

# O5 — JAWOYN MUSIC STICKS / BARUNGA-BAMYILI, NORTHERN TERRITORY, AUSTRALIA

## Source grounding

Museums Victoria documents a 1976 pair of music sticks made by Jawoyn artist Bob Maldubal. The museum states that the set is **made in pairs** and used to accompany singing and dancing by striking the flatter stick with the more circular form.

Source:
- https://collections.museumsvictoria.com.au/items/241962

## Documented relationship

Two distinct wooden members form a percussion pair. One strikes the other and the contact generates the rhythmic pulse used in musical/ceremonial accompaniment.

## Gap-map territory

Primary: **R5 / R10 — Contact Impact**
Secondary: temporal pulse.

## Candidate law

# **CONTACT BETWEEN A PAIRED STRIKER AND BASE STICK PRODUCES RHYTHMIC PULSE.**

Signature:
`STICK A ≠ STICK B → APPROACH → STRIKE → PULSE → REPEAT`

## Pair translation

- Card A = flatter/base music stick;
- Card B = more circular striker;
- direct user strike produces one short pulse;
- repeated timing remains user-authored rather than auto-played.

## Strengths

- exceptionally literal two-object pair;
- source is community-specific rather than pan-Aboriginal;
- direct interaction is obvious and build complexity is low;
- no third physical object required for the immediate sound event.

## Risks

- collection already contains Ombak and Siku, so another sound family must contribute more than “two objects make sound”;
- ceremonial context requires restraint and community-specific naming;
- visual imagery may require cultural clearances;
- without a richer relational law the family could feel too mechanically simple for one of only two open Oceania slots.

# O6 — GARAMUT + WOODEN POUNDER / EAST SEPIK-RAMU, PAPUA NEW GUINEA

## Source grounding

The National Gallery of Australia documents garamut slit drums from Papua New Guinea as being pounded with blunt sticks to create complex rhythms capable of transmitting information over long distances.

The Fralin Museum of Art, in an exhibition developed with Papua New Guinean scholarship and museum consultation, describes garamut as resonant bodies beaten with a wooden pounder to announce meetings, issue warnings and communicate with neighboring villages through complex rhythms and tones.

An indigenous-knowledge paper from the University of Papua New Guinea documents garamut signaling in Maramba society as a traditional communication medium.

Sources:
- https://digital.nga.gov.au/archive/exhibition/mythmagic/default.cfm?irn=211455&bioartistirn=211455&mystartrow=61&realstartrow=61&mnuid=3&galid=0&viewid=2
- https://uvafralinartmuseum.virginia.edu/exhibitions/voices-connection-garamut-slit-drums-new-guinea
- https://pngbuai.com/600technology/information/waigani/drums/WS97-sec7-Norman2.html

## Documented relationship

A wooden pounder strikes the resonant body. The meaningful outcome is not merely sound volume: ordered rhythm/tone patterns can function as signals carrying information across distance.

## Gap-map territory

Primary: **R11 — Encode / Signal**
Secondary: impact/contact.

## Candidate law

# **ORDERED IMPACTS ON A GARAMUT TURN RHYTHM INTO A LONG-DISTANCE SIGNAL.**

Signature:
`POUNDER ≠ GARAMUT → STRIKE → RHYTHM → SIGNAL → CARRY`

## Pair translation

- Card A = `WOODEN POUNDER / INPUT`;
- Card B = `GARAMUT / RESONANT SIGNAL BODY`;
- direct strikes create a user-authored pulse sequence;
- an editorial signal lane may record timing, but must never claim an invented sequence is an authentic community code;
- the center remains proof metadata, not a third communication device.

## Strengths

- opens the rare R11 encode/signal territory;
- direct physical pair with immediate cause/effect;
- source explains the difference between generic percussion and communicative rhythm;
- PNG anchor adds geographic diversity to Oceania.

## Risks

# **HIGH CULTURAL-CONTEXT DISCIPLINE REQUIRED.**

Garamut can be sacred/restricted, and meanings, naming and playing rules differ by community. Some source contexts involve gender restrictions and initiation.

Therefore:
- choose one defensible regional/community source context at spec stage;
- do not reproduce or invent an “authentic” coded warning/death/ceremonial message;
- do not copy carved clan/spirit imagery as decorative surface treatment;
- a build should demonstrate `impact sequence → encoded signal concept`, not teach restricted codes.

Sound-category overlap with Ombak/Siku exists at the medium level, but the causal law is materially different: communication encoding rather than beating or melodic alternation.

## Preliminary status

# **VERY STRONG MECHANISM / HIGH-CONTEXT DEEP REVIEW**
