# SOURCE AUDIT

## 1. Museo Nacional de Antropología / INAH — Teotitlán del Valle

Official MNA/INAH documentation specifically connects the metate to the Zapotec community of Teotitlán del Valle, Valles Centrales de Oaxaca.

Documented cues:
- the metate is an important domestic instrument in Teotitlán del Valle;
- it is connected to household and matrimonial life and may form part of a marriage dowry/gift;
- it is used in food preparation;
- for cacao preparation, a worn metate may be warmed by embers beneath it so the grind becomes more plastic / workable.

Source:
`https://mna.inah.gob.mx/detalle_pieza_mes.php?id=252`

R5 implication:
Teotitlán specificity should come from the documented object/use context and stone-food process, not generic Zapotec decorative motifs.

## 2. National Museum of the American Indian — Oaxaca metate + mano

NMAI object records document paired metate/flat mortar and mano/grinding stone objects from Oaxaca.

Documented cues:
- paired lower grinding surface + upper grinding stone;
- stone / scoria material;
- ground/carved construction;
- food gathering/preparation function.

Sources:
`https://americanindian.si.edu/collections-search/object/NMAI_127113`
`https://americanindian.si.edu/collections-search/object/NMAI_279471`

R5 implication:
The pair should remain visibly stone-on-stone and material-facing; the memorable event should emerge from repeated reciprocal contact.

## 3. Teotitlán contemporary documentation

A reusable Commons photograph documents indigo being ground on a metate in a Teotitlán del Valle workshop. This supports continued non-food material grinding context in the locality without requiring it to become the candidate's central claim.

Source:
`https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:GutierrezWorkshopTeotitlan075.jpg`

## MATCHING

1. both stones exist independently;
2. B approaches and registers contact with A;
3. repeated forward/back reversal occurs across the working lane;
4. each reversal adds visible abrasion traces rather than just moving B;
5. coarse particles fragment and spread into a progressively finer material field;
6. a fine-ground band accumulates on A only because B repeatedly works against it;
7. center/contact effects remain subordinate.
