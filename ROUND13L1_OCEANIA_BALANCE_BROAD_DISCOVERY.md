# ROUND 13L.1 — OCEANIA #2 / BROAD DISCOVERY

Date: 2026-08-25
Branch: `round13l/oceania-balance-discovery`

## Gate objective

Find a second Oceania family that increases geographic balance **without repeating Marshall Islands Swell** and without turning culture into decorative skin.

Canonical invariant:

**THE RELATIONAL PAIR REMAINS THE PRODUCT.**

Existing Oceania family already occupies:
- Marshall Islands / Swell Register
- environmental wave deflection
- navigation / orientation
- relation to ocean swell

Therefore 13L should prefer a materially different mechanism.

## Broad candidate field

### TIER A — advance to deep qualification

## 1. Aotearoa / Māori — Hika Ahi / fire-plough pair

Documented mechanism:
- pointed `hika` / fire-making stick;
- grooved `kauahi` / base stick;
- repeated back-and-forth friction;
- heat accumulates in wood dust;
- a small ember can form at the end of the groove.

Authoritative grounding:
- Te Papa, `Hika Ahi – Making Fire`
- Te Ara, Māori fire-making kit / making fire
- Science Museum Group fire-plough record

Candidate law:

**RECIPROCAL FRICTION RELEASES LATENT HEAT INTO EMBER.**

Proof idea:
`HIKA + KAUAHI → FRICTION → HEAT → EMBER`

Pair-native strength:
VERY HIGH. Two distinct tool elements interact directly; the ember is an emergent state produced at their interface.

Primary risk:
Fire-making is a culturally specific practice and the prototype must not become a practical fire-starting tutorial. Exact ignition timing, temperatures and wood calibration must remain non-claimed/editorial.

Visual-rights note:
Te Papa and Te Ara historical images are restricted. Science Museum Group has a CC BY-NC-SA photograph. A freely reusable historical or tool-focused source still needs to be located before implementation, or the interface must use an original editorial rendering grounded in the sources rather than copying restricted imagery.

---

## 2. Aboriginal Australia — Millstone + Muller / Grindstone pair

Documented mechanism:
- larger lower stone / millstone holds material;
- smaller upper stone / muller is worked against it;
- seeds can be mechanically crushed into flour;
- the same broad tool class also supports pigments, resins and other processing.

Authoritative grounding:
- Australian Museum, `Grindstones`
- Australian Museum, `Food Culture: Aboriginal Bread`
- Australian Museum, Wailwan grindstone / Unsettled material

Candidate law:

**RECIPROCAL ABRASION TURNS WHOLE MATERIAL INTO PREPARED MATTER.**

Proof idea:
`MULLER + MILLSTONE → PRESSURE / MOTION → PARTICLE TRANSFORMATION`

Pair-native strength:
HIGH. Two physically distinct stones have complementary roles, although the processed seed/pigment is a third material in the causal system.

Primary risk:
Could drift toward another Food Memory if framed only around flour. The stronger family would be about **abrasion / granulation / preparation**, not cooking or eating.

Visual-rights note:
Australian Museum imagery is generally copyrighted; source grounding is strong but a reusable visual anchor must be solved separately.

---

## 3. Polynesia — Double-Hulled Canoe / Twin Hull Structure

Documented mechanism:
- two hulls are lashed / joined in parallel;
- two hulls increase stability and seaworthiness;
- double hulls also increase carrying capacity;
- Polynesian examples include Tongan, Samoan, Tahitian, Hawaiian and other regional forms.

Authoritative grounding:
- Te Ara, `Polynesian double hull`
- Te Ara, `Waka – canoes / Pacific origins`
- Te Papa, `Pacific canoes`
- Archaeology in Oceania literature on sailing performance

Candidate law:

**DISTRIBUTED BUOYANCY PRODUCES STABILITY.**

Proof idea:
`HULL A + HULL B → JOIN → DISTRIBUTE → STABILIZE`

Pair-native strength:
VERY HIGH. Two large physical bodies remain distinct while producing a structural property neither hull provides in the same way alone.

Primary risk:
Maritime context may feel too close to Marshall Islands Swell. It must be framed as **structural coupling / load distribution**, never navigation, swell reading or route finding.

Visual-rights note:
A 2025 Wikimedia Commons image of Polynesian double-hulled canoes is CC BY-SA 4.0 and could provide a usable visual anchor. Historical Tasman sketches are source-rich but reuse rights can be restricted by holding institutions.

---

## 4. Sāmoa / Tahiti / West Polynesia — Tapa Beater + Anvil

Documented mechanism:
- inner bark is placed on a wooden anvil;
- a wooden tapa beater repeatedly pounds / flattens the bark;
- beating widens the material into a larger barkcloth surface;
- tool terminology varies across Pacific cultures (`i‘e`, `ike`, `tutua`, etc.).

Authoritative grounding:
- Te Papa, Sāmoan `i‘e (tapa beater)`
- Te Papa, Tahitian `tutua (tapa beating anvil)`
- Te Papa, `Tapa: barkcloth art in the Pacific`

Candidate law:

**REPEATED COMPRESSION TURNS A STRIP INTO A SURFACE.**

Proof idea:
`BEATER + ANVIL → IMPACT → SPREAD → CLOTH SURFACE`

Pair-native strength:
HIGH-MEDIUM. The beater and anvil are a true tool pair, but the bark substrate is a necessary third material.

Primary risk:
Could become generic craft process or overlap Print/Kento if the design over-emphasizes pattern. The relational law must remain **compression / widening**, not decorative transfer.

Visual-rights note:
Te Papa tool photographs frequently carry restricted rights; usable object imagery would need a separate rights solution or original source-grounded rendering.

---

### TIER B — preserve, lower priority

## 5. Aotearoa / Māori — Paired Poi

Documented mechanism:
- poi are kinetic percussion instruments / performance objects;
- performers may use a single poi or a pair;
- paired movement can generate complex visual and rhythmic patterns.

Candidate law:
**COUNTER-MOTION PRODUCES A SHARED PATTERN.**

Why Tier B:
The pair is culturally real, but a single poi is also a valid practice. Pair-native necessity is therefore weaker than Hika Ahi or twin hulls, and the family would re-enter sound/rhythm territory after Ombak and Siku.

## 6. Aotearoa / Māori — Toki + Hoanga / Grinding Relation

Documented mechanism:
- toki/adze preforms are finished by grinding on sandstone / hoanga, often with water;
- rubbing smooths the blade and sharpens the cutting edge.

Candidate law:
**CONTROLLED ABRASION PRODUCES AN EDGE.**

Why Tier B:
Strong material causality, but conceptually adjacent to the Australian grindstone candidate and less balanced as two co-equal product objects because one object is being worked into its final state.

## Anti-duplication comparison

- Swell: environment/wave → orientation. 13L candidates above avoid this except twin-hull, which must stay structural.
- Khipu: cord/tension/knot. None of the Tier A candidates use this law.
- Fès: material fit/tessellation. None rely on geometric fit.
- Kento: block/paper transfer. Tapa must avoid decoration/print framing.
- Ombak: simultaneous acoustic difference. Poi is downgraded partly for this reason.
- Siku: alternating distributed notes. None of the Tier A candidates use alternation as the core law.
- Binocular: controlled disparity/depth. No optical overlap.
