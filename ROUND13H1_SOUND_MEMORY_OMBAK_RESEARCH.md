## Edition

## Promoted mechanism
**OMBAK REGISTER**

A relationship becomes valid not when two tones become identical, but when their intentional difference produces the intended shared beat.

Interaction:
`TONE → APPROACH → DIFFERENCE → BEAT → REGISTER → RESONATE → CONTINUE`

## Source evidence
### Smithsonian National Museum of Asian Art
Lightbulb Ensemble: The Acoustic Concept of an American Gamelan
https://asia.si.edu/research/essays/baumbusch/

Relevant evidence:
- ombak is a frequency-beating relationship between nearby pitches;
- pengisup is the higher-pitched member of a pair;
- pengumbang is the lower-pitched member;
- common Bali practice can maintain a shared ombak rate through the ensemble;
- gong kebyar is documented as often using an ombak around 7 Hz, while other gamelan types may use different rates.

### Smithsonian concert notes
Balinese Music and Dance: Gamelan Mitra Kusuma
https://asia-archive.si.edu/podcast/balinese-music-and-dance-gamelan-mitra-kusuma/

Relevant evidence:
- the characteristic shimmering sound is created by matched instruments tuned slightly differently from each other.

## Truthfulness boundary
- Do not claim one universal ombak rate for all Balinese gamelan.
- The prototype's 440/447 Hz pair is an editorial synthesis chosen to demonstrate a 7 Hz beat.
- The 440/452 Hz mismatch is also editorial and not a historical measurement.
- No synthesized tone is presented as archival gamelan audio.
- No photographed instrument is claimed to have the displayed frequencies.
