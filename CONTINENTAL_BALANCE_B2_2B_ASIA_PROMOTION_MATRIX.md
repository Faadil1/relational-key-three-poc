# FINALIST A — PERSIAN PLANISPHERIC ASTROLABE / ISFAHAN, IRAN

## Evidence strengthened

Smithsonian documents Persian planispheric astrolabes made in Isfahan and elsewhere in Iran. A ca. 1715 example includes five plates, a rete/star map, alidade, central pin and other components. The Smithsonian identifies the rete as a star map and the plates beneath it as altitude/azimuth grids for specific latitudes.

Oxford History of Science Museum documentation explicitly states that on a planispheric astrolabe the **rete rotates over the stationary latitude plate**. By rotating the rete relative to the plate, the user can model the apparent revolution of the firmament relative to a local horizon.

British Museum OA+.369 provides an additional Safavid Iranian anchor dated 1712 with a rete and seven latitude plates.

Preferred visual anchor for later build research:
- Smithsonian Persian planispheric astrolabe, Isfahan, ca. 1715;
- Smithsonian metadata marks the object media as public domain / open access where available.

Primary sources:
- https://www.si.edu/object/persian-planispheric-astrolabe:nmah_997138
- https://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object/nmah_997148
- https://www.mhs.ox.ac.uk/astrolabe/catalogue/browseReport/Astrolabe_ID=209.html
- https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/W_OA-369

## Honest reduction

The complete historical instrument contains more than two parts. RELATIONAL KEY must not claim otherwise.

The family would isolate one documented functional relation:

# `RETE ↔ LATITUDE PLATE`

That relation is sufficient to express relative rotation and celestial/horizon reading while explicitly labelling the edition as a structural translation of one astrolabe subsystem.

# FINALIST B — CHINESE TIMBER TENON-JOINT CRAFTSMANSHIP / CHINA

## Evidence strengthened

UNESCO's 2009 Representative List entry states that Chinese traditional timber-framed structures connect columns, beams, purlins, lintels and bracket sets through tenon joints, creating flexible and earthquake-resistant frames assembled from prefabricated components.

The Palace Museum also documents traditional architectural/furniture joinery vocabulary, but current research has not yet isolated one single representative pair-joint that is both culturally specific enough and safe to present as the family-native mechanism.

Primary sources:
- https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/chinese-traditional-architectural-craftsmanship-for-timber-framed-structures-00223

`MEMBER A + MEMBER B → ENGAGE → HOLD / FLEX`

# FINALIST C — JEONGNANG COMMUNICATING GATE / JEJU, KOREA

## Evidence strengthened

National Folk Museum of Korea documentation states that `jeongjumok` gate posts in Jeju have holes for long wooden `jeongnang` bars. Placing the bars across the gate prevents livestock entry and also indicates that the owner is out.

Jeju Special Self-Governing Province educational material classifies Jeongnang with keywords including three long logs, homeowner, visitor, implicit agreement and outing signal.

Official Visit Jeju material further documents traditional interpretations in which the number of bars engaged communicates different degrees/durations of homeowner absence.

Primary sources:
- https://www.nfm.go.kr/kids/user/content.do?pageId=PAGE_000000000000015
- https://www.jeju.go.kr/jedu/data/data.htm?act=view&category=3100&page=11&seq=1538265
- https://visitjeju.net/en/themtour/view?contentsid=CNTS_200000000010595
- https://jeju.museum.go.kr/html/en/sub03/sub03_0302.html

## Candidate law

# **BAR CONFIGURATION TURNS A GATE INTO A MESSAGE.**

Signature:

`BARS ≠ POSTS → ENGAGE 0–3 → GATE STATE → DECODE AVAILABILITY`

## Strength

This is not merely information displayed on a cultural object. The same physical engagement simultaneously changes:
- access control;

That physical/informational duality is highly distinctive.

## Main unresolved issue

The pair architecture requires discipline because Card A contains three bars rather than one simple member. The design must ensure the bar set belongs to Card A and the receiving posts belong to Card B; the gap must not become a standalone dashboard/code reader.

There is also moderate conceptual proximity to Service Register because both communicate state. Jeongnang remains defensible only if physical gate engagement, not textual status display, is the interaction.
