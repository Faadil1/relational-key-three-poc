# A1 — PERSIAN PLANISPHERIC ASTROLABE / IRAN

## Source grounding

Primary institutional anchors:
- British Museum, Safavid astrolabe made in Iran, 1712, museum no. OA+.369.
- Smithsonian National Museum of American History, Persian planispheric astrolabes including 1372/73 and ca. 1715 examples.
- Oxford History of Science Museum astrolabe research/catalogue explaining that the rete rotates over the stationary latitude plate.

Source URLs:
- https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/W_OA-369
- https://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object/nmah_997147
- https://www.mhs.ox.ac.uk/astrolabe/catalogue/browseReport/Astrolabe_ID=209.html

## Documented relationship

An astrolabe combines a **rete** — an openwork star map — with one or more engraved latitude plates held in a mater. The rete rotates relative to the plate to model the apparent rotation of the heavens and support astronomical/time calculations.

## Gap-map territory

Primary: **R2 — Hinge / Rotation / Articulation**
Secondary: **R12 — Calibrate / Tune / Measure**

## Candidate relational law

# `ROTATING A STAR MAP AGAINST A LOCAL HORIZON PRODUCES A CELESTIAL READING.`

## Pair translation

- Card A = `RETE / STAR MAP`
- Card B = `LATITUDE PLATE / LOCAL HORIZON`
- relationship = controlled rotation around a shared axis;
- matching/calibrated state = selected celestial/time reading becomes legible;
- alternate rotation remains a different valid reading, not a broken card.

## Strengths

- extremely high mechanism novelty relative to current Asia trio;
- genuine relation is rotational/calibrational, not decorative astronomy;
- visually rich openwork geometry without requiring stereotyped Persian ornament;
- strong museum documentation;
- alternate states are naturally valid rather than binary right/wrong.

## Risks

- real astrolabes contain more than two components; the digital reduction must explicitly state that it isolates the rete ↔ latitude-plate relation rather than claiming to reproduce the entire instrument;
- avoid turning the overlapping circular center into a standalone decorative product;
- keep scientific/astronomical framing; do not overclaim religious use as the defining meaning.

# A2 — CHINESE TIMBER TENON-JOINT CRAFTSMANSHIP / CHINA

## Source grounding

UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage:
`Chinese traditional architectural craftsmanship for timber-framed structures`, inscribed 2009.

UNESCO states that columns, beams, purlins, lintels and bracket sets are connected by tenon joints in flexible, earthquake-resistant timber frames assembled from prefabricated components.

Source URLs:
- https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/chinese-traditional-architectural-craftsmanship-for-timber-framed-structures-00223
- https://ich.unesco.org/en/individual-case-study-00988?id=00024

## Documented relationship

Prefabricated wooden structural members engage through tenon-joint geometry to create a strong flexible frame.

## Gap-map territory

Primary: **R5 — Latch / Clasp / Discrete Engagement**
Secondary: **R4 — Load / Stack / Accumulation**

## Candidate relational law

# `ENGAGED JOINT GEOMETRY PRODUCES STRUCTURAL CONTINUITY.`

Possible stronger formulation after deeper source review:
`PRECISE ENGAGEMENT ALLOWS A FRAME TO HOLD AND FLEX.`

## Pair translation

- Card A = one structural member carrying a tenon;
- Card B = receiving structural member / mortise geometry;
- approach must happen with correct orientation;
- engagement produces a visible structural lock;
- post-engagement load/flex test demonstrates why the relation matters;
- nonmatching orientation leaves a residual gap without invalidating either member.

## Strengths

- source authority is exceptionally strong;
- pair-native physical relationship is immediate;
- engagement + post-engagement load behavior can distinguish it from passive tessellation;
- culturally specific through a documented living architectural craft rather than applied ornament.

## Risks

- could collapse into `Zellige 2.0` if reduced to "shape fits hole";
- must prove **engagement + structural behavior**, not merely visual fit;
- select a defensible representative joint rather than presenting one simplified joint as the totality of Chinese timber architecture.

## Preliminary status

# **VERY STRONG / DEEP SOURCE GATE**

# A3 — JEONGNANG COMMUNICATING GATE / JEJU, KOREA

## Source grounding

Institutional / official anchors:
- Jeju National Museum lists `jeongnang` as gates used at private homes in its outdoor exhibition.
- Visit Jeju, Jeju's official tourism platform, documents the traditional three-log gate and describes how the number of logs engaged across the entrance communicated the homeowner's absence / distance.

Source URLs:
- https://jeju.museum.go.kr/html/en/sub03/sub03_0302.html
- https://visitjeju.net/en/themtour/view?contentsid=CNTS_200000000010595
- https://www.visitjeju.net/en/themtour/view?contentsid=CNTS_300000000012488&menuId=DOM_000002000000000221

## Documented relationship

Three wooden logs can be placed between gate frames. Their engaged/unengaged configuration functions both as a physical gate and as a message about whether the homeowner is present or away.

## Gap-map territory

Primary: **R11 — Encode / Decode / Completion**
Secondary: **R5 — Latch / Discrete Engagement**

## Candidate relational law

# `BAR ENGAGEMENT COUNT TURNS A GATE INTO A MESSAGE.`

Alternative:
# `POSITIONED BARS PRODUCE SOCIAL AVAILABILITY INFORMATION.`

## Pair translation

- Card A = three bounded gate bars belonging visibly to one member;
- Card B = receiving gate-frame / jeongjumok geometry;
- insert 0–3 bars;
- each configuration decodes to a different documented availability state;
- all configurations are valid states rather than match/fail.

## Strengths

- highly legible physical + informational dual function;
- culturally specific and geographically precise;
- interaction is not merely decorative coding: engagement physically changes the gate;
- introduces a strong R11 encode/decode topology absent from current Asia.

## Risks

- relation uses three bars within Card A, so pair-native composition needs careful discipline;
- meanings should be framed as documented traditional Jeju interpretations, not universal timeless rules for every household;
- has some conceptual proximity to Service Register because both communicate state, so physical engagement must remain central.

# A4 — JOSEON MOON JAR / KOREA

## Source grounding

National Museum of Korea and Metropolitan Museum of Art both document that large Joseon moon jars were typically made by forming upper and lower hemispheres separately and joining them. The seam and firing could create subtle asymmetry.

Source URLs:
- https://www.museum.go.kr/ENG/contents/E0201040400.do?relicId=27894&schM=view&showHallId=759
- https://www.museum.go.kr/ENG/contents/E0401000000.do?relicRecommendId=519669&schM=view
- https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/45432

## Documented relationship

Two separately thrown porcelain hemispheres are joined to create the large jar; the joining line may remain visible and contributes to the organic asymmetry of the result.

## Gap-map territory

Primary: **R4 — Load / Stack / Accumulation**
Secondary: material joining / seam behavior.

## Candidate relational law

# `TWO SEPARATELY FORMED HEMISPHERES JOIN THROUGH A VISIBLE SEAM.`

## Pair translation

- Card A = upper formed hemisphere;
- Card B = lower formed hemisphere;
- vertical approach / join rather than side-by-side alignment;
- seam remains visible after joining;
- small offsets can remain valid and produce organic asymmetry.

## Strengths

- exceptionally clear museum documentation;
- strong Korean specificity;
- seam imperfection provides a sophisticated alternative to perfect matching;
- new vertical relationship topology for the collection.

## Risks

# **ANTI-DUPLICATION RISK IS MATERIAL.**

If translated poorly, the interaction becomes exactly what the Gap Map warns against: "two halves complete one picture/object."

The whole vessel could also become more visually dominant than the two source members, violating `THE PAIR IS THE PRODUCT`.

## Preliminary status

# **WATCH / BEAUTIFUL SOURCE, PAIR-PRODUCT RISK**

# A5 — GIREUMTEUL STONE OIL PRESS / JEJU, KOREA

## Source grounding

Jeju National Museum describes `Gireumteul`, an oil press for rapeseed or sesame oil, as composed of a top stone and bottom stone. Pressing the top stone extracts oil; a round groove in the bottom stone collects it. The large press was communal village property.

Source URL:
- https://jeju.museum.go.kr/html/en/sub03/ajax/ajax_popup_07.html

## Documented relationship

Top stone applies pressure; bottom stone receives the load and channels the extracted oil.

## Gap-map territory

Primary: **R10 — Compression / Expansion / Stored Force**
Secondary: **R7 — Transfer / Pour / Containment**

## Candidate relational law

# `PRESSURE BETWEEN TWO STONES RELEASES AND CHANNELS OIL.`

## Pair translation

- Card A = top press stone;
- Card B = grooved receiving stone;
- vertical compression creates extraction;
- lower member channels the resulting flow;
- incomplete compression yields partial extraction rather than binary failure.

## Strengths

- true two-member force relationship;
- underrepresented compression topology;
- source-grounded daily/community technology rather than decorative motif;
- visually different from every current Asian family.

## Risks

- current web evidence is concise; deeper technical documentation is needed before promotion;
- ensure the physical historical press is not oversimplified if additional components were essential in actual operation;
- second Korean/Jeju candidate may reduce geographic breadth even if mechanism is strong.

## Preliminary status

# **WATCH / SOURCE DEPTH REQUIRED**

# A6 — MAETDOL / KOREAN ROTARY STONE QUERN

## Source grounding

- Smithsonian field documentation describes a Korean `Maettol` stone quern with a top revolving stone and fixed lower stone, coarse interfacing surfaces, and a feed hole for material to be ground.
- Jeju National Museum includes maetdol/millstones in its outdoor collection.

Source URLs:
- Smithsonian Korean village material-culture study: https://repository.si.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/85b859fd-6321-48d2-b13d-42966e4e6c64/content
- https://jeju.museum.go.kr/html/en/sub03/sub03_0302.html

## Documented relationship

The upper stone rotates against a fixed lower stone; material is introduced through the upper stone and ground between the coarse facing surfaces.

## Gap-map territory

Primary: **R2 — Rotation / Articulation**
Secondary: **R10 — Compression / Stored Force**

## Candidate relational law

# `RELATIVE ROTATION BETWEEN TWO STONES PRODUCES GRINDING.`

## Strengths

- extremely clear pair-native mechanics;
- interaction would be immediately understandable;
- rotation/compression is absent from current Asian trio.

## Risks

- rotary querns are widespread across many cultures, reducing Asia-specific distinctiveness;
- another Korean candidate in an already Korea-heavy pool;
- less culturally singular than astrolabe, Jeongnang or Chinese timber craftsmanship.

## Preliminary status

# **RESERVE / MECHANICALLY STRONG, SPECIFICITY WEAKER**
