# Authoritative source anchors

## National Gallery of Australia — Garamut drum

The NGA documents an East Sepik / Ramu River garamut carved from a hollowed tree trunk and states that:
- it is pounded with blunt sticks;
- complex rhythms can act as communicative / codified language;
- sound can transmit information over long distances between villages.

Source:
https://digital.nga.gov.au/archive/exhibition/mythmagic/default.cfm?irn=211455&bioartistirn=211455&mystartrow=61&realstartrow=61&mnuid=3&galid=0&viewid=2

## Fralin Museum of Art — Voices of Connection

The Fralin Museum describes garamut as:
- a resonant body beaten with a wooden pounder;
- used for rhythm, announcements and communication with neighboring villages;
- capable of conveying communication through complex rhythms and tones.

The exhibition was developed with Papua New Guinean scholarly/museum consultation, including Andrew Moutu, former director of Papua New Guinea’s National Museum & Art Gallery.

Source:
https://uvafralinartmuseum.virginia.edu/exhibitions/voices-connection-garamut-slit-drums-new-guinea

## University of Papua New Guinea — indigenous-knowledge documentation

Paul Yamngarpise Norman documents garamut/tapr signaling in Maramba society from indigenous knowledge and identifies the drum as a traditional communication medium.

Source:
https://pngbuai.com/600technology/information/waigani/drums/WS97-sec7-Norman2.html

# Direct strike interaction

## Initial state

- Card A pounder visible;
- Card B garamut visible;
- no event trace;
- state `NO IMPACT EVENTS RECORDED`.

## Strike motion

The user directly drags the pounder toward the garamut strike zone.

A strike event is credited only when:
1. the pounder crosses the editorial contact threshold;
2. the previous strike has been released/withdrawn enough to re-arm.

This hysteresis/debounce prevents pointer jitter from generating false multiple hits.

## Event capture

Each credited strike records:
- event order;
- relative timestamp from the first event;
- interval from previous event where applicable.

After one strike:
`ONE IMPACT · NO SEQUENCE CLAIM YET`.

After two or more:
`ORDERED EVENTS · EDITORIAL SIGNAL SEQUENCE`.

No terminal success state exists.

No sequence is “correct” or “incorrect”.

## Withdraw / re-arm

The user must move the pounder away from the contact threshold before another strike can be captured.

This makes each event visibly caused by a distinct approach/contact cycle.

## Reset

Reset:
- clears event history;
- returns pounder to offset state;
- stops any active synthetic audio;
- restores `NO IMPACT EVENTS RECORDED`.
