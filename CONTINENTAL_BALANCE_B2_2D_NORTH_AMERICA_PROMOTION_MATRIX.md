# FINALIST N1 — JANNEY AUTOMATIC RAIL COUPLER / UNITED STATES

## Deep-source evidence

Smithsonian National Museum of American History preserves Janney coupler objects/models including a ca. 1870 model.

Original U.S. Patent 138,405 (April 29, 1873) describes a car coupling using a hook/knuckle, catch and guard arm. The patent states that compatible parts interlock closely as cars come together and can be uncoupled by releasing the lever.

USPTO classification documentation describes the Janney type as a coupling head with a guard arm and pivoted angular knuckle with means to lock the knuckle in coupling position.

Sources:
- https://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object/nmah_881750
- https://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object/nmah_841985
- https://patents.google.com/patent/US138405A/en
- https://www.uspto.gov/web/patents/classification/uspc213/defs213.pdf

# FINALIST N2 — YALE PIN-TUMBLER LOCK / UNITED STATES

## Deep-source evidence

Smithsonian National Museum of American History preserves an 1861 lock patent model associated with Linus Yale Jr. and identifies it under mechanisms.

Mechanism documentation explains that a serrated flat key lifts varying pin stacks to positions permitting the plug to rotate.

Sources:
- https://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object/nmah_850663
- https://www.yaledws.co.uk/uk/en/about-us/history-of-yale/the-yale-pin-tumbler-invention
- https://www.lockmuseumofamerica.org/museum-rooms

## Primary relational identity

# **R11 — PHYSICAL ENCODE / DECODE**

Secondary:
R5 key engagement.

## Candidate law

# **KEY PROFILE POSITIONS PIN STACKS SO A CYLINDER CAN ROTATE.**

Signature:
`KEY ≠ LOCK → INSERT → LIFT → SHEAR LINE → ROTATE → WITHDRAW`

## Strength

This is one of the cleanest encode/decode laws in the full candidate pool. OTHER-key semantics are naturally valid: the key can remain correct for a different lock while leaving residual pin crossings in this one.

## Why it is not promoted now

Yale is strong enough to become a family in another context, but promoting it alongside Janney would give North America's two new slots a shared broad visual/action language:

`metal mechanism → approach/insert → discrete engagement → lock → release`

Even though their primary topologies differ (`R11` versus `R5`), the continental pair would feel more repetitive than necessary.

It also creates a second U.S. industrial-mechanism family while leaving daily/material technologies underrepresented.

# FINALIST N3 — METATE + METLAPIL / MEXICO

## Deep-source evidence

Museo Nacional de Antropología / INAH documents a `Metate zapoteco` and explicitly states that discussing a metate requires discussing its complement, the cylindrical elongated `metlapil`. The museum text notes the Nahuatl-derived expression as meaning roughly **two that grind something**, making complementarity intrinsic to the object language rather than an invented RELATIONAL KEY metaphor.

INAH also documents metates and manos/metlapillis in archaeological site collections.

Smithsonian NMAI preserves a paired `Metate/Flat mortar and mano/grinding stone` associated with Kikapu (Mexican Kickapoo), Coahuila.

Sources:
- https://mna.inah.gob.mx/detalle_pieza_mes.php?id=252
- https://americanindian.si.edu/collections-search/object/NMAI_28704
- https://inah.gob.mx/museos/222-museo-de-sitio-de-higueras
- https://lugares.inah.gob.mx/es/node/4312

## Primary relational identity

# **R10 — PRESSURE + RECIPROCAL ABRASION**

Secondary:
material transformation through accumulated movement.

## Candidate law

# **PRESSURE AND RECIPROCAL STROKE BETWEEN COMPLEMENTARY STONES PRODUCE GRINDING.**

Signature:
`METLAPIL ≠ METATE → LOAD → STROKE → ABRADE → GRIND STATE → CONTINUE`

## Pair proof

- source literature itself frames the two implements as complementary;
- moving member and receiving surface remain visibly distinct;
- output changes gradually through repeated relation rather than binary success;
- pressure and stroke are both required, giving a two-variable physical interaction distinct from Toyama reveal;
- alternate stroke/load states can remain valid, producing different progression rather than invalidity.

## Cultural framing boundary

The family must not claim that the grinding mechanism is uniquely Zapotec, Kikapu or uniquely Mexican: related grinding technologies are widespread.

Instead:
- choose a specific museum object/context as the visual/historical anchor;
- state that RELATIONAL KEY is translating the documented complementary metate ↔ metlapil relation;
- avoid copying decorative carving as cultural skin;
- do not generalize one object's ceremonial/domestic context to all Mesoamerican communities.

# FINALIST N4 — MAYA CALENDAR ROUND

## Deep-source evidence

Smithsonian National Museum of the American Indian's `Living Maya Time` project documents:
- Tzolk'in / Chol Q'ij 260-day cycle;
- Haab 365-day cycle;
- Calendar Round as their interweaving;
- a given paired combination repeating after 18,980 days / 52 Haab years.

The same source is unequivocal that Maya calendar knowledge remains living. It includes contemporary Maya voices, Day Keepers, agricultural cycles, ceremonies and descriptions of time as sacred.

Sources:
- https://maya.nmai.si.edu/calendar/calendar-system
- https://maya.nmai.si.edu/sites/default/files/resources/The%20Maya%20Calendar%20System.pdf
- https://maya.nmai.si.edu/corn-and-maya-time
- https://maya.nmai.si.edu/the-maya/maya-people

## Primary relational identity

# **R9 — INDEPENDENT CYCLE SYNCHRONIZATION**

## Candidate law

# **TWO INDEPENDENT CALENDAR CYCLES PRODUCE A DATE THROUGH SYNCHRONIZATION.**

Mechanically this is excellent and remains distinct from Siku and Ombak.

## Why it is not promoted

The source material demonstrates that reducing this system to a visually attractive pair of rotating calendar wheels risks extracting a living sacred/calendar-keeper knowledge system from its community context.

RELATIONAL KEY does not need this mechanism badly enough to justify that reduction while equally strong lower-risk candidates exist.

This is therefore not a quality rejection; it is a **context-respect decision**.
