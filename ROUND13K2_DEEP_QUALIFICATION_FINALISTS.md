# A — BOLIVIA / AYMARA SIKU — IRA + ARKA

## Verified mechanism

Smithsonian Folkways documents the Andean panpipe construction as two complementary single-row pipes. The two parts are played by two individuals in alternating / hocketing fashion and together produce a single scale and melody.

Universidad de Chile MAPA independently describes the siku as dual and complementary: the scale is distributed between arka and ira, which are played as a pair and alternate sounds to weave a melody.

The Metropolitan Museum of Art separately catalogs Bolivian `Siku (ira)` and `Siku (arca)` instruments from Italaque, Bolivia.

## Native pair status

**FULLY PAIR-NATIVE.**

The two complementary instruments exist as separate physical objects and are played by separate musicians. Neither half contains the complete scale.

This is not an editorial pair invented for the interface.

## Relational law

**COMPLEMENTARY ABSENCE PRODUCES CONTINUITY.**

Proof:
`IRA ≠ ARKA → ALTERNATION → ONE SCALE / ONE MELODY`

Potential signature:
`NOTE → GAP → ANSWER → INTERLOCK → SCALE → MELODY → CONTINUE`

## Distinction from Ombak

Ombak:
`SIMULTANEOUS NEAR-UNISON DIFFERENCE → BEAT / RESONANCE`

Siku:
`DISTRIBUTED NON-OVERLAPPING NOTES → ALTERNATION → COMPLETE MELODY`

The medium overlaps sound, but the relational law does not.

## Visual evidence

Strong reusable visual anchor exists:
- Wikimedia Commons `Siku bipolar.jpg` — explicitly shows the separated Arka and Ira halves; released into the public domain.
- Additional Bolivian performance photography exists under CC0 / Creative Commons.

## Implementation truthfulness

A prototype may synthesize simple illustrative tones, but must not claim archival or instrument-accurate timbre unless directly sourced.

The interface may encode a short note sequence to demonstrate alternation; it must not claim that the displayed melody is an authenticated traditional piece unless sourced.

## Verdict

**FINALIST A — PROMOTE TO CONCEPT GATE.**

Strongest pair-native candidate in the entire Round 13K field.

# B — COLOMBIA / PASTO VARNISH MOPA-MOPA

## Verified mechanism

UNESCO recognizes the traditional knowledge and techniques associated with Pasto Varnish mopa-mopa of Putumayo and Nariño.

Museo Colonial documents the material process: mopa-mopa resin is cleaned and heated, pigmented, kneaded and stretched into a thin sheet, adhered to the object, then defined with a sharp tool. Multiple layers of different colors form the final decoration.

## Native pair status

**MATERIAL-PAIR NATIVE / PRODUCT-PAIR EDITORIAL.**

Distinct resin layers exist physically during the craft process, but the historical output is a decorated object rather than two separate card-like objects.

A RELATIONAL KEY translation can truthfully use:
- Card A — upper mopa-mopa color layer;
- Card B — lower layer / receiving ground;
- Gap — overlap / cut-reveal field.

This must be labeled an editorial translation of the documented layering process.

## Relational law

**A LAYER BECOMES INFORMATION THROUGH WHAT IT REVEALS BENEATH IT.**

Potential signature:
`HEAT → STRETCH → OVERLAY → CUT → REVEAL → REGISTER → CONTINUE`

## Distinction from Kento

Kento:
`BLOCK + PAPER → ALIGNMENT → PRESS → TRANSFER`

Mopa-Mopa:
`COLOR LAYER + UNDERLAYER → OVERLAY → CUT → REVEAL`

Both involve material-on-surface processes, but Pasto's proof is subtractive / layered reveal rather than registration-based transfer.

## Visual evidence

Usable cultural visual anchors exist on Wikimedia Commons:
- Barniz de Pasto detail by Óscar O. Granja Peña — CC BY-SA 3.0.
- multiple Pasto varnish objects and process images under CC BY-SA licenses.

These are strong enough for a source-first prototype if attribution / share-alike requirements are respected.

## Implementation truthfulness

Do not imply the digital cut path reproduces a historic artisan pattern unless it is directly sourced.

Do not imply that two physical cards were part of the historical technique.

The safest claim is that the interaction translates the documented logic of heated resin sheets, layering, cutting and color reveal.

## Verdict

**FINALIST B — PROMOTE TO CONCEPT GATE.**

Strongest non-sound candidate and strongest material transformation in the field.

# C — BRAZIL / PERNAMBUCO / COBOGÓ

## Verified mechanism

Brazilian institutional and museum sources ground the cobogó as a perforated modular architectural element created in Pernambuco / Recife in the late 1920s. It admits light and ventilation, provides partial privacy, and creates graphic shadow effects as light passes through the openings.

## Native pair status

**SEMI-PAIR-NATIVE / TRIADIC CAUSALITY.**

The screen is a physical object, but the emergent shadow requires both incident light and a receiving surface. The most truthful pair would therefore be screen + receiving field, with light acting as an external input.

This is structurally less pure than Siku.

## Relational law

**PERFORATION TURNS LIGHT INTO SPATIAL INFORMATION.**

Potential signature:
`SCREEN → LIGHT → FILTER → PROJECT → SHADOW → REGISTER`

## Distinction from Swell

Swell transforms a moving environmental wave field through deflection around an atoll.

Cobogó transforms incident light through fixed apertures into patterned projection and simultaneously admits air.

The mechanism is distinct, but both rely on environmental inputs.

## Visual evidence

Excellent Creative Commons anchors exist, including cobogó facades in Recife under CC BY 2.0 and other Brazilian examples under CC BY-SA.

## Verdict

**RESERVE #1 — DO NOT ELIMINATE.**

Exceptional visual candidate, but the product pair is less native and the environmental-input overlap makes it weaker than the two finalists for 13K.

# D — ARGENTINA / URUGUAY / MATE + BOMBILLA

## Verified mechanism

Argentina's cultural heritage register documents mate as yerba in a vessel with hot water consumed through a bombilla.

The Smithsonian NMAI has public-domain bombilla collection material, and the British Museum catalogs bombillas explicitly as `mate-strainer` objects.

The submerged end functions as a strainer / filter while also acting as the drinking tube.

## Native pair status

**FUNCTIONALLY PAIR-NATIVE.**

Mate vessel and bombilla are distinct physical objects and are culturally legible as a set.

The functional proof, however, depends on a third material field: yerba + infusion.

## Relational law

**SELECTIVE PASSAGE PRODUCES A CLEAN CONTINUATION.**

Potential signature:
`INFUSE → APPROACH → FILTER → EXCLUDE → FLOW → CONTINUE`

## Visual evidence

Strong usable material exists:
- Smithsonian bombilla media in public domain;
- Buenos Aires government mate imagery under CC BY 2.5 Argentina;
- historical Uruguay mate/bombilla collection imagery with public-domain status.

## Verdict

**RESERVE #2.**

Very clean functional pair and strong visual-source availability, but the relational outcome is less conceptually distinctive than Siku and less materially surprising than Pasto Varnish.
