## Scope

## Documented evolution

### 2022 — first youth free-access initiative
Source: STO news release, 31 May 2022.
- Unlimited free transit for Gatineau secondary students during July–August.
- Existing student Multi cards could be activated and new “Libre-accès jeunesse” cards were distributed through 17 secondary schools and STO service points.
- The youth title supported interoperability with OC Transpo and Transcollines for that summer.
- The program was explicitly framed around autonomy, environmental habits, access to jobs/activities, and learning public transit before driver-licence age.
Source: https://www.sto.ca/actualites/cet-ete-la-sto-facilite-lacces-au-transport-en-commun-a-plus-de-15-000-jeunes-de-niveau-secondaire/

### 2023 — repetition turns the offer into a recognizable youth program
Source: STO news release, 19 May 2023; STO promotion-extension release.
- Second consecutive summer.
- Distribution through 18 secondary schools.
- STO described the card in the field as a “carte Multi aux couleurs du libre-accès”.
- Youth mobility was framed as freedom, self-reliance, orientation, openness to others, and access to local activities/work.
Source: https://www.sto.ca/actualites/retour-du-libre-acces-jeunesse-a-gatineau-les-etudiants-de-niveau-secondaire-invites-a-prendre-dassaut-le-reseau/
Source: https://www.sto.ca/actualites/deux-promotions-prolongees-afin-de-faciliter-les-deplacements-sur-le-reseau-de-transport-en-commun-a-gatineau/

### 2024 — new permanent Multi Jeunesse object
Source: STO news release.
- The program becomes year-round rather than summer-only.
- A new Multi Jeunesse card is introduced.
- STO explicitly describes it as having a “nouveau look vivant” with youth and regional icons.
- Around 19,000 students / 18 secondary schools are in scope.
- The card is photo-less, associated to the school profile, and valid for all five years of secondary school.
- The no-photo approach is described as more durable and simpler to distribute/manage.
- The same object can also carry a monthly pass or electronic purse and can be linked for web reload.
Source: https://www.sto.ca/actualites/carte-multi-jeunesse-une-mobilite-durable-pour-tous-les-eleves-des-ecoles-secondaires-de-gatineau-a-lannee/

### 2026 — same civic object, personal ownership, mandatory validation
Sources: current Multi Jeunesse page and FAQ.
- All students receive the same card and are told to write their full name on the back.
- The card is intended to be kept for the whole secondary-school journey.
- It can serve integrated school transport, free-access periods, monthly pass, and electronic purse.
- It is accepted on STO and OC Transpo, while Transcollines is currently outside the acceptance boundary.
- The card must still be presented/validated when the fare is $0.
- STO explicitly explains that validation at zero fare supports passage data and helps build the habit of validating on entry.
Source: https://sto.ca/tarifs/multi-jeunesse/
Source: https://www.sto.ca/infos-pratiques/faq-foire-aux-questions/

## Evidence-backed mechanism candidates

### M1 — COMMON OBJECT / PERSONAL MARK
Evidence: every student receives the same Multi Jeunesse; the user writes their name on the back.
Design implication: identity can emerge through a controlled personal mark without replacing the common civic object.
Do not infer: open-ended personalization is not documented.

### M2 — FIVE-YEAR COMPANION
Evidence: the same card is meant to remain with the student through secondary school.
Design implication: longevity and progression can influence hierarchy/material/series logic.
Do not infer: the card does not visibly accumulate five years of events today.

### M3 — FREE DOES NOT MEAN INVISIBLE
Evidence: validation is still required when fare = $0.
Design implication: entitlement and action remain separate; a free relationship still produces a meaningful registration event.
This strongly fits Registered Continuation.

### M4 — SCHOOL → CITY AUTONOMY
Evidence: the program is repeatedly framed around freedom, autonomy, orientation, jobs, activities and learning transit before driving age.
Design implication: the object can represent a threshold from dependent mobility to independent civic navigation.

### M5 — LOCAL ICONS AS CIVIC BELONGING
Evidence: STO describes the 2024 card as using youth and regional icons.
Design implication: local visual material belongs on the object, but only after its source set is identified and translated structurally.
Do not infer: no individual icon meaning has yet been verified in this corpus.

### M6 — ONE OBJECT, MULTIPLE ENTITLEMENT MODES
Evidence: free-access windows, integrated school transport, monthly subscription and electronic purse can coexist on the same object.
Design implication: one credential can carry multiple contextual claims without becoming a benefits dashboard.

### M7 — NETWORK BOUNDARY IS PART OF THE RELATIONSHIP
Evidence: STO/OC Transpo acceptance versus current Transcollines non-acceptance.
Design implication: the same valid credential can produce different outcomes by destination/network relationship; this directly reinforces RELATIONAL KEY.

## Strongest current collisions

1. FREE DOES NOT MEAN INVISIBLE × REGISTERED CONTINUATION
   - The most direct product fit.
   - A zero-cost entitlement still requires a real validation/registration event.

2. COMMON OBJECT / PERSONAL MARK × FIVE-YEAR COMPANION
   - A shared civic object becomes personally owned through use and duration, without needing a portrait.

3. SCHOOL → CITY AUTONOMY × NETWORK BOUNDARY
   - Youth learns that mobility is relational: access depends on where/when/network, not simply whether the card is “valid”.

## Early concept territories — NOT YET PROMOTED

### A — ZERO FARE / REAL PASSAGE
Signature idea: a relationship can resolve to $0 and still demand registration.
Potential unique geometry: the junction closes first; only then does the fare field collapse to zero while the path remains active.
Risk: can become too explanatory/technical if handled literally.

### B — FIVE-YEAR CIVIC COMPANION
Signature idea: one shared card accompanies the threshold from Secondary 1 to graduation.
Potential unique geometry: persistent spine / five restrained phase coordinates, without pretending the card currently records milestones.
Risk: avoid fake progression data.

### C — FIRST INDEPENDENT ROUTE
Signature idea: the credential marks the transition from being transported by others to navigating the city independently.
Potential unique geometry: a path that begins constrained and becomes self-directed after registration.
Risk: must remain a credential system, not a route-planner metaphor.
